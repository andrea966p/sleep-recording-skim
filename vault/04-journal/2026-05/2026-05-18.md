## Focus

Investigate Diane's "lost weekend recording," recover what's possible, and close the underlying bug. Side-effect: build the real-time half of the chain-of-custody safety net the [[2026-05-01-llm-council-review#Chairman Synthesis — Verbatim|May 1 council]] called for.

## TL;DR

- The "48 h weekend recording" was actually **three 95.5 h sessions May 7 → May 11** for Diane / H5S251A mice, all marked `failed: export timed out` in their JSON sidecars.
- Three independent failures stacked: a hidden **8.3 h recording stall** on May 8, the daemon's **5-minute export-wait cap**, and an **orphaned export ffmpeg** for pi_cam_2.
- Recovered the two surviving files (~5.8 GB each, 8.3 h of valid H.264). pi_cam_2 is unrecoverable — Frigate's 7-day retention pruned the source segments before today.
- Shipped two fixes: progress-driven export wait ([[export-timeout-fix|guide]]) and a per-camera recording-health watchdog ([[recording-health-watchdog|guide]]).
- End-to-end validated against a real `systemctl stop go2rtc` on Pi1 — watchdog fired STALL at lag=68s, recovered at lag=13s. Export pipeline now completes 9-min sessions in 10 s instead of timing out.

## What Diane reported

> "Last weekend I ran more than 48 h video recording from 2 cages. When I stopped the video from Frigate manually, the export didn't happen. Don't see the video exported in the folder and from the Tauri UI it says export error."

## Findings (root causes)

Three failures stacked; recollection was off on every dimension:

| Claimed | Actual |
| --- | --- |
| last weekend (May 16–17) | **May 7 → May 11** |
| > 48 h | **~95.5 h per session** |
| 2 cages | **3 cages** — pi_cam_0 (H5S251A_MIX), pi_cam_1 (H5S251A_WT), pi_cam_2 (H5S251A_MUT) |

### Failure 1 — Export wait timed out at 5 min

`frigate_controller.py:26` had `EXPORT_TIMEOUT = 300.0`. For 95.5 h sessions, Frigate's multiplexing took ~6–7 min. The daemon gave up at 5 min, wrote `"failed: export timed out"`, and **never called `_move_export_file()`**. Frigate finished anyway and left the files orphaned, root-owned, in `/srv/frigate/storage/exports/`:

```
pi_cam_0_20260507_174431-20260511_171813_izz943.mp4   5.78 GB
pi_cam_1_20260507_174436-20260511_171818_nnw1z8.mp4   5.93 GB
```

Distinct from the [[2026-03-06|Mar 6 race-condition fix]]: that one moved files too early; this one gave up too early.

### Failure 2 — Recording silently stalled on May 8

Even with the export fix, only **~30 000 s = 8.3 h** of content existed for those two cameras (vs. 95.5 h requested). Both pi_cam_0 and pi_cam_1 cut off at the *same wall-clock instant* (~02:04 KST May 8, 8 h 20 m into the session). They're both on Pi1.

Forensics on every component:

| Component | Status during window |
| --- | --- |
| Pi1 host uptime | 16 d 19 h — no reboot |
| Pi1 `go2rtc.service` | Continuously active since 2026-05-01 09:33 BST |
| Pi1 journal at 18:00–18:15 BST May 7 (= 02:00–02:15 KST May 8) | Nothing — completely quiet |
| NVR journal at 02:04 KST May 8 | Cron / sysstat / tailscale only — no Frigate, no docker events |
| Frigate container | No restart between May 7 and May 18 12:20 |
| Frigate Python log for May 7–11 | **GONE** — `/dev/shm/logs/frigate/current` is tmpfs, wiped on today's 12:20 container restart |

The most likely root cause (Frigate's per-camera ffmpeg stalling without auto-restart) is consistent with the symptom, but the actual evidence was wiped. **The watchdog now closes this evidence gap.**

### Failure 3 — pi_cam_2 export ffmpeg orphaned, never produced a file

The export DB row for pi_cam_2 sat at `in_progress = 1` for a week. Frigate's `/api/exports` started returning HTTP 500 fifteen seconds after the third concurrent export was POSTed (signs of subsystem contention). When today's container restart killed the zombie ffmpeg, the cleanup logic in `record/export.py:run()` — which only fires on `subprocess.run()` return — never ran, leaving the orphan row + dangling thumb.

Cleaned via `DELETE /api/export/pi_cam_2_py3do3`. Confirmed no remaining `in_progress = 1` rows.

## Fixes shipped

### `frigate_controller.py` — progress-driven `wait_for_export()`

Replaced the fixed 5-min deadline with: as long as Frigate reports `in_progress=True`, keep waiting. Stall grace 10 min if the export disappears or Frigate becomes unreachable; 24 h absolute ceiling.

Validated today: 9-min test session exported in **10 s** vs. the old 300 s ceiling.

Guide: [[export-timeout-fix]] (status: completed).

### `recording_watchdog.py` — new module

Background thread polls Frigate's `recordings` SQLite table every 30 s. For each camera in an active manual or Radiens session, alerts (log + MQTT) if `MAX(end_time)` lags wall clock by > 60 s. 30 s start-grace to avoid false positives on session boot. Surfaces per-camera lag on `/api/health` for future UI consumption.

Validated end-to-end:

```
14:37:21  Manual recording started (mouse=trial_recording_cras)
            ↓
            ssh skimlab@pi1 'sudo systemctl stop go2rtc'    ← real outage
            ↓
14:41:36  ERROR: Recording STALLED on pi_cam_0: lag=68s
            ↓
            sudo systemctl start go2rtc
            ↓
14:44:06  WARNING: Recording recovered on pi_cam_0 (lag now 13s)
```

One STALL line per event (not spammy), correct recovery, lag value on `/api/health.recording_lag`. Guide: [[recording-health-watchdog]] (status: completed; MQTT→Slack wiring + UI badge deferred).

## System-wide view — what this buys us

The May 1 council's central thesis was: **the most dangerous failures in this system are the silent ones, and the alarms watch CPU instead of provenance.** Today's incident is exactly that pattern — three sessions, ~287 hours of intended recording, marked `failed` while ~17 GB of usable footage sat in a directory the daemon never checked. No alarm fired. Diane noticed by visual inspection of the export folder.

Two specific reframes from the council carry through:

- **Contrarian's "Apr 8 stuck export is integrity masquerading as reliability":** today's pi_cam_2 export was the same class of bug — DB says `in_progress=True`, file says it doesn't exist, daemon says `failed: export timed out`. The progress-driven wait closes the reliability shell; the watchdog is the first piece of real-time integrity instrumentation this system has had.
- **Reviewers' "the alarms watch CPU, not provenance":** Netdata never saw any of this. Uptime Kuma never saw any of this. The first thing that did see it was a query against Frigate's own segment table. That query now runs every 30 s.

Two things the watchdog is *not* a substitute for:

1. **The append-only journal (Stage 2.1).** The watchdog watches Frigate's view of segments — it can't catch the daemon's own metadata loss (e.g. a manual session whose JSON never made it to disk because of the manual-sessions-persisted-only-at-stop pattern). The journal still needs to happen.
2. **The nightly reconciler (Stage 2.2).** Retrospective vs. real-time. Watchdog catches stalls within ~90 s while a session is live; reconciler catches orphan MP4s and missing-export discrepancies after the fact. Both are needed — different failure horizons.

## Next steps

Roughly in order of leverage. Items marked **STAGE-N** map to [[2026-05-01-llm-council-review#The Staged Plan]].

### Still pending from May 1 (highest leverage)

- **STAGE-1**: `apt-mark hold` Docker packages, Mosquitto bind/auth, Syncthing exclude, Frigate image tag pin, repo push to remote. None of this happened today. The 5-min `apt-mark hold` is *still* the highest reliability-per-minute item in the system.
- **STAGE-2.1**: Append-only SQLite session journal. Today's investigation would have been far cheaper with this in place — we'd know exactly when pi_cam_0 stopped writing, not have to infer it from a 30 000 s file duration.
- **STAGE-2.2**: Nightly reconciler — diffs Frigate `recordings` against session journal, Slack alert on orphans.

### New items surfaced by this investigation

- **Persist Frigate's Python log to a non-tmpfs path.** The single most useful piece of evidence for understanding the May 8 stall — Frigate's per-camera ffmpeg state log — was wiped by today's container restart. Mounting `/dev/shm/logs` (or just `/dev/shm/logs/frigate`) to `/srv/frigate/logs/` in `docker-compose.yml` would have preserved it. Cheap. ~10 min change. **High leverage** because the watchdog now ensures we'll see future stalls within 90 s of occurring — but only useful if Frigate's own log survives long enough to look at.
- **Sanity-check exported duration against session duration.** After `wait_for_export` succeeds, run `ffprobe` on the moved file. If duration < 95 % of `session.duration_seconds`, set `export_status` accordingly (e.g. `"completed (partial: 8.3h of 95.5h)"`) so the discrepancy surfaces in the UI instead of looking green. Today's recovered files use this format manually; mechanise it.
- **Chunk long exports by day boundary.** A 95 h ffmpeg concat job is itself the failure surface that orphaned pi_cam_2's export. Sessions > 24 h could export day-by-day. Lower blast radius per ffmpeg job; partial loss instead of total loss when one chunk fails.
- **Watchdog → MQTT → Netdata → Slack.** The watchdog already publishes `neurosurveillance/watchdog/{camera}/stall`; Netdata's MQTT collector subscription is the missing link before alerts reach a human. ~30 min wiring + one alert rule in `health.d/nvr-alerts.conf`.
- **UI badge for `recording_lag`.** `/api/health.recording_lag` is now populated; frontend just needs to read it. Pattern mirrors the existing camera-offline badge in `ChamberSilo`.

### Deliberately deferred

- Auto-recovery on watchdog stall (MQTT OFF→ON toggle to force Frigate to restart its ffmpeg). Need to see the watchdog fire on at least one organic real-world stall first before automating remediation — heisenbugs love auto-restarts.
- TTL pulse upgrade and all of STAGE-3. Council was unanimous: stabilise integrity before changing the sync primitive.
- Expansionist platform play (detection, DeepLabCut, JOSS). Same reason as May 1.

## Open question

**What actually stopped the recording on Pi1 cameras at 02:04 KST May 8?**

Pi up, go2rtc up, NVR up, Frigate up, network quiet. The only system component whose log was wiped today is Frigate's per-camera capture process. If a similar stall happens again, the watchdog will fire within 90 s — *but only if Frigate's log survives* the next container restart so we can read it. That's why "persist Frigate's log" is the highest-priority item on the new-items list above.
