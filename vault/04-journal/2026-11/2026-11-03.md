# 2026-11-03: HLB EEG re-scored, 15 paired mice instead of 3

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> - All 64 HLB EEG mouse-windows were re-scored from the per-mouse CSVs on the SKIM NAS, with
>   sha256 checked and no counter gaps. Results: [[hlb-eeg-sleep-overview]].
> - **15 mice are now usable for W / NR / R at both timepoints** (W-SD 1, S-SD 1, W-SL 7, S-SL 6),
>   against 3 under the old verdicts. The old screen was rejecting good mice, as Andrea suspected.
> - **No detectable SOD1 effect on baseline sleep** (52.0 vs 52.5 %, n = 10 vs 11, MDE 6 points), nor on
>   the recovery change without deprivation. The deprivation arms are still one mouse each.
> - New vault section `10-projects/hlb-sod1/eeg-sleep/`: overview, method, data backbone, runbook, one note per mouse.
> - Checked against Andrea's reviewed HLBSSL33 R epochs (light phase, from the workstation backup): κ 0.952; the rule-free HMM κ 0.930.

## What was done

- **Method** ([[hlb-eeg-scoring-method-2026-11]]):
  - The professor's SleepStager rule, unchanged, fed an EMG measured between the 60 Hz harmonics.
  - Second scorer: a per-recording HMM. Single-signal scorers: EEG alone and EMG alone.
  - Label-free usability tests per hour and per recording, giving tiers A to X. Bars were written
    before the screen and calibrated on the positive control HLBSSL33 R only.
- **Compute** ([[hlb-eeg-compute-runbook]]):
  - The ThinkPad, venv `~/hlb-eeg-venv`, data in `~/sleep-data/hlb-rescore-2026-11/`.
  - The office PC's GPU was offered but not needed: nothing is trained, and the bottleneck was the NAS.
- **Independent code review** before the cohort run. It found 60 Hz itself un-notched in the gap
  filter, a tier C that could never be reached and a wrong `r_1min` definition. All fixed and recomputed.
- **Agent review** (Workflow, 13 agents):
  - 8 Sonnet scorers read every overview and staged 456 contested epochs (59 % agreed with the rule).
  - 4 Opus skeptics tried to refute the 36 newly usable or tier C windows. One critic checked for gaps.
- **What the review changed:**
  - Three screen flaws, fixed in code with all 64 re-scored: dead EEG had passed tier C, the
    EEG-alone HMM split at technical steps, and near all-sleep hours were dropped.
  - 7 borderline windows demoted by evidence. None were promoted.
- **Data backbone for figures** ([[hlb-eeg-data-backbone]]): long tables of epochs, hours, bouts,
  spectra and the NREM δ time course, in `~/sleep-data/hlb-rescore-2026-11/backbone/`.

## Results worth remembering

- HLBSSD18 (the only S-SD pair) gains +18 points of recovery sleep, but only +1.5 if its EMG-rail
  epochs (27 %, mostly vigorous wake) count as wake. No S-SD vs W-SD reading.
- HLBWSD08 (the only W-SD pair) gains +9.3 points, robust to that choice. NREM δ rebounds to 114 %.
- The EEG-alone scorer agrees with the rule (NREM κ median 0.86, range 0.73–0.89, over the 38 A/B windows; rule vs HMM κ median 0.88), and gives the same
  baseline answer (40.8 vs 40.9 % NREM).

## Incident: a background wait hung for about 6 hours

The overnight chain was meant to rescore, then continue unattended. Its wait loop
(`while pgrep -f 'score.py /home'`) matched its own command line and had no deadline, so nothing
ran for about 6 hours, until Andrea checked in the morning. Scoring itself had finished. The
memory rule on background waits was tightened: steps are now chained with `&&` in one command, every
wait gets a deadline, and `pgrep -f` / `pkill -f` must never match their own line.
