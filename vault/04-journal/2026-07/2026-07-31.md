# 2026-07-31 — Electricians came, earth did nothing, and the electrodes turned out to be OPEN rather than badly implanted

> [!danger] ⚠️ MAJOR RETRACTION 2026-08-01 — THE CHANNEL MAP USED HERE WAS WRONG
> This document was written using `EEG = native0 − native1` and `EMG = native2 − native3`. **Both are wrong.** The RHD2216 is BIPOLAR: `native0` (Allego `/1`) is ALREADY the EEG, `native1` (`/2`) is ALREADY the EMG, and natives 2/3 (`/3`,`/4`) are **empty pads with no wire on them by design**.
>
> **What is RETRACTED from this document:**
> - ❌ "EMG failed 23/24 / 16/16 legs" — those were **empty pads**, never electrodes. **EMG works on 5 of 8 mice.** The surgeries were fine.
> - ❌ "ZERO scoreable mice / zero scoreable data" — there are **21.0 scoreable mouse-hours** (C1 8.7 h, C2 7.0 h, D2 5.3 h).
> - ❌ "C1 shows NO sleep structure" — C1 shows **textbook sleep architecture**: WAKE 66.8 %, NREM 29.3 %, REM 3.9 %, all physiology correct, persistence r=+0.80 at 1 min.
> - ❌ Duty cycle "78.7 % open" — **61.6 %** on the 16 real channels.
> - ❌ The "frontal vs parietal" framing — it was **EEG vs EMG channels**.
>
> **What STANDS:** the no-wire control method, the grounds result (2.8 µV, no drift over 27.9 h), environmental causes closed, and contact intermittency / one-way degradation — though it is the **EEG** channel that fails, on 8 of 8 mice.
>
> Full correction: [[2026-08-01-channel-mapping-correction-first-sleep]]


Long day. Started with electricians standing in the room waiting to hear whether their earth wiring fixed the 60 Hz hum, ended with the project's standing conclusion overturned and the first clean EEG pair we have ever recorded. Full write up in [[2026-07-31-contact-intermittency-no-wire-control]], reusable method in [[electrode-connection-state-test]].

## Headlines

- ❌ **The earth wiring changed nothing measurable.** Released the electricians. Keep the bond, it is correct practice, but earth stays EXONERATED.
- ✅ **The SPI splitter is now excluded by DIRECT PHYSICAL TEST**, not inference. Pulled every splitter, ran it again, same result.
- ✅ **All 8 animal grounds are good.** 128 internally tied control pins at a median of 7.2 µV. The amplifier floor here is about 7 µV, so there is NO elevated common mode and NO environmental problem left to chase.
- 🔴 **22 of 32 electrode legs are ELECTRICALLY OPEN**, meaning statistically indistinguishable from an amplifier input with NO WIRE ATTACHED.
- 🔴 **Contact state FLIPS SPONTANEOUSLY.** Caught one leg dropping 522× in half a second with nobody touching the rig.
- ⭐ **C1 gave us the first clean unclipped EEG pair in the project.** 0.00 % clipping, 11.7 µV of mains hum on the bipolar, delta dominant, sane 1/f falloff.
- 🛑 **DO NOT schedule new surgeries.** The old "make new mice" guidance is not supported by today's evidence.
- ⚠️ **Impedance snapshots are no longer a valid go or no go gate.**

## How the day went

### Morning, the electricians

They had bonded the XDAQ chassis ground to the wall earth and wanted a verdict. Two mice on port B, channels 1 to 4 and 33 to 36, one minute recording plus an impedance sweep.

First pass I measured 60 Hz on a single FFT bin and reported the low impedance legs looked clean at 23 to 42 µV. **That was wrong and I corrected it within the hour.** Mains is not exactly 60.00 Hz and it drifts, so a single bin smears and understates the hum by 4 to 5×. Measured properly as 55 to 65 Hz band power, the best leg was 154 µV, not 42.

The verdict did not change though: no demonstrable improvement, and the best legs were nowhere near clean. Told them to keep the bond and go home.

### Midday, the splitter goes on trial and is acquitted

Andrea ran mouse 1 alone with the splitter still attached, then mouse 2 alone with the splitter fully removed. Mouse 2 without the splitter produced the WORST legs of the whole dataset, 7167 and 7383 µV, one of them clipping 14 % of samples. Removing the splitter made nothing better.

Later, with all four splitters physically pulled, the 16 remaining legs sat at a Z median of 4.226 MΩ against a no wire control of 4.764 MΩ. **Splitter closed.**

### The measurement that cracked it

The impedance CSV exports contain far more than the mouse legs, and I finally used the rest of them. Every port carries channels whose headstage pads have NOTHING attached, sites 5 to 16 and 37 to 48. That is a physically guaranteed open circuit, measured by the same instrument, same session, same chip.

```
CONTROL A, NO WIRE ATTACHED (n=96) : Z median 5.233 MΩ,  mains 2400 µV
YOUR 32 MOUSE LEGS                 : Z median 5.329 MΩ,  mains 2667 µV
CONTROL B, internally tied (n=128) : Z median 0.043 MΩ,  mains    7.2 µV
```

The median mouse leg is indistinguishable from a channel with nothing plugged into it. Not "high impedance", not "a poor electrode". **OPEN.**

For months we read 5 to 8 MΩ and called it a bad implant. Nobody had ever measured what no connection at all reads on this hardware. It reads 5.233 MΩ.

### Then I watched one break, live

`B2 parietal`, mid recording, nobody touching anything:

```
60 Hz envelope, 10 s blocks (µV):
  4346  4369  3485  |  7  8  8  9  9
```

Transition at t = 28.02 s, spanning 0.5 seconds, 522× drop. Before: rms 575 µV, 12.5 % clipping, open and acting as an antenna. After: rms 5.8 µV with no biology, the amplifier floor. **It went from OPEN to SHORTED.** Both broken, differently.

⚠️ Tissue does not do that in half a second. That is metal losing contact with metal.

This finally explains why every impedance snapshot in this project has disagreed with the last one. One leg moved **102× in eleven minutes**. We were sampling a switch that flips on its own.

### Evening, C1 and the wiggle test

C1's EEG pair came out clean: 0.00 % clipping, 15 µV of mains hum on the bipolar, delta 50.3 %, theta 18.8 %, 1/f ratio 8.7.

> [!note] This is NOT another 2026-07-15
> The retracted "first sleep biology" was a clipping artifact. **This channel clips exactly 0.00 % of samples.** That is precisely the failure that forced the retraction and it is absent here.

Then Andrea provoked C1 while streaming, one joint at a time: electrode 10 to 20 s, tether 30 to 40 s, slip ring wires 50 to 60 s, twisted the slip ring 70 to 80 s. C1 frontal stayed between 2.2 and 30.3 µV across the whole 101 seconds, median 11.6. **Nothing moved it.** Bipolar over the full run: rms 65.0 µV, mains 11.7 µV, 0.00 % clipping, theta up at 27.7 % which fits an animal being handled.

**A properly made connection on this rig is ROBUST.** That is a reference standard we did not have.

### ⚠️ And a retraction, same day

During that test `B1 frontal` started chattering at about t = 45 s and never recovered, bouncing between roughly 10 and 4400 µV for the last 55 seconds. A1 and D1 did not move at all, so it was not body coupling.

I read that as a physical path between C1's slip ring and B1's signal chain and wrote that the fault was localised to the slip ring assembly. **Andrea confirmed B1 and C1 share nothing. RETRACTED.** The timing was coincidence. Given `B2 parietal` flipped with nobody touching anything, spontaneous flips clearly happen on their own, and B1's chatter is most likely another one.

Cost of the retraction: **the fault is NOT localised.** C1's electrode, tether and slip ring are cleared on that animal, but I have no positive evidence pointing at any specific joint elsewhere.

## Other things settled today

- **The Allego notch does NOT reach the saved data.** Parked it at 50 Hz where nothing real lives and checked the saved spectrum: 1 Hz bins across 47 to 53 Hz came out 2.457, 2.504, 2.354, 2.401, 2.365, 2.462, perfectly flat, no null. `.xdat` is raw, hardware bandpass only. This had been an open question and it retroactively validates every analysis in the session.
- **The two headstage map procedures are IDENTICAL.** [[allego-electrode-setup-sleep64]] insists on `usrdef_sleep64`, [[eight-mouse-acquisition-setup]] says `Passthrough 64`. I diffed them: the same 64 native to site pairs, both pure passthrough. The md5 differs only because one writes floats and the other ints. **Either is correct.** The sleep64 warning was aimed at the archived repacking maps, not at `Passthrough 64`.
- **For one headstage per port with no splitter, use `Passthrough 32` + `Default 4x8`**, verified pure passthrough and confirmed as what Allego itself picks.
- **EMG failed 23 of 24 legs** across two independent hardware configurations. That is systematic and it is its own problem, separate from the EEG story.
- **ZERO fully scoreable mice today.** A scoreable animal needs a clean EEG pair AND a working EMG pair. C1 has the EEG and not the EMG.
- Allego crashed once after the splitter unplug and re enumeration. Restart cleared it, consistent with the known backend crash on rapid sensor and channel churn documented in [[signal-mapping-guide]] §10.

## Still open

- **What causes the flips.** Connector micro motion, thermal cycling, or something at the electrode tissue interface. Cannot separate them yet.
- **Whether C1 holds for hours**, or whether 101 s was a long lucky interval.
- Flip rate and duty cycle per leg, which is the number that tells us how many scoreable mouse hours this rig actually yields.
- Whether flips cluster in time, which would suggest a shared cause.
- Why EMG fails at 23 of 24.
- **Is the Allego impedance check itself trustworthy?** Never validated against a known resistor. Five minute test: 1 MΩ between a headstage input and its GND pad, see if it reads 1 MΩ.
- [[experiment-pad7-floating-input-B1]] is now more interesting, since B1 is the animal that chattered and it is the one with an unconnected slip ring wire sitting in a live amplifier input.
