# 2026-07-05 — 🔴 THE BIG ONE: Korea mains is 60 Hz, not 50 Hz. Weeks of "bad electrodes" were mostly 60 Hz hum + a wrong notch.

> [!danger] TL;DR — we were chasing the wrong problem
> All this time the notch was set to **50 Hz** and every signal-quality call assumed 50 Hz. **South Korea mains is 60 Hz.** The real interference (60 Hz) was never filtered. Single channels drowning in ~10 mV of 60 Hz common-mode hum looked "railed / open / bad contact," and we concluded the mice were badly implanted. **Wrong.** It's **one systematic environmental problem**, not 8 bad surgeries. With a **60 Hz notch + bipolar difference**, real EEG comes back on **5 of 8 mice right now**. Full explainer: [[60hz-mains-hum-discovery]].

## How it surfaced

Operator pushed back — *"impossible all 8 mice have poor connection"* — and asked me to think like an outsider. So instead of thresholding single-channel amplitude (what I'd wrongly been doing), I looked at the **actual waveform**:

- **Dominant frequency = 59.9 Hz on every channel**, clean and "railing" alike. That's mains — and it's **60 Hz**, not 50.
- The "railing" 11–12 mV pk-pk is **~100 % 60 Hz hum** (≈0 % in the 1–45 Hz EEG band), i.e. **common-mode mains**, not open-input broadband noise.
- The recording is **bipolar** (EEG = frontal − parietal); the difference cancels common-mode hum. I'd been judging **single legs**, not the pair, AND the notch was on the wrong line.

## Proof

After applying a **60 Hz notch + bipolar difference (pad0−pad1)** to a live 8.6 s pull, all 8 mice, 4-port C3442:

| Mouse | recovered EEG (µV) | leg clipping | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 (A1) | 310 | 0 % | usable |
| M3 | 202 | 8 % | usable |
| M4 | 56, delta-rich | 5 % | clear real EEG |
| M5 | 134 | 1 % | usable |
| M6 | 703 | 4 % | usable |
| M2 | 84 | 17 % | clipped (fix hum) |
| M7 | 133 | 16 % | clipped |
| M8 | 364 | 11 % | clipped |

Common-mode 60 Hz per electrode ranged **15 µV → ~4900 µV**, tracking **ground quality**: A1 (good ground) rejects it (~88 µV) and gives clean EEG; noisy animals need a better ground, not a new implant.

## What it means

- The mice are **not** all badly implanted. The dominant limiter was **60 Hz + wrong notch** — systematic, environmental, fixable.
- **Record + postprocess is viable now** for the 5 non-clipping mice (operator was right).
- **Clipping is the only real loss:** where 60 Hz exceeds the amp's ±6.4 mV range the samples saturate and are gone — no filter recovers them. ~3/8 mice have clipping legs → reduce hum at source first.
