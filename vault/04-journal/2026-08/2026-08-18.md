Eight newly operated mice from batch **W-SD** went on the rig and I took a short recording,

⚠️ **THESE ARE EIGHT BRAND NEW ANIMALS AND NOTHING FROM ANY EARLIER COHORT CARRIES OVER.** A rig
position is a socket on the amplifier, not an identity, so "D2" today and "D2" in an older note
are different mice. Every judgement was made against wire free amplifier inputs recorded inside
this same file.

**Five of the eight give clean brain signal: W-SD A1, A2, B2, C1 and D2, all at 100 % of epochs
with zero railing.** Four of the five also give a believable muscle signal; **W-SD B2 has
electroencephalogram (EEG) only**, its electromyogram (EMG) channel sits at 475 µV with 640 µV of
mains hum and is open. **W-SD C2 has an open EEG electrode**, quiet and spectrally flat at the
same time, which is the signature of an input attached to nothing.

Full numbers, tables and the reasoning: [[2026-08-18-W-SD-cohort-first-screen]].

### 🔴 Two headstages lost their ground TODAY

**W-SD B1 and W-SD D1 cannot be judged at all**, because the unconnected inputs on their own chips
have gone silent: 162 µV and 82 µV of 60 Hz, against 3000 to 5200 µV on the other six chips. A
grounded chip sees the room's mains hum on its bare inputs; a chip with no ground rejects mains as
common mode and goes quiet. Their control has stopped being a control.

Comparing the same table across the last four recordings shows the silent chip was **port A slot 2
on 14 and 16 August**, and today port A slot 2 is normal while B slot 1 and D slot 1 have gone
silent instead. **The fault follows whatever was last unplugged**, which makes it the same ground
pin problem as [[2026-08-07]] and a connector re mate, with no anaesthesia. Andrea is checking the
seating tomorrow morning.

🔑 **Verify that fix on the 60 Hz of the wire free inputs, NOT on an impedance sweep.** A floating
channel reads a beautifully low and stable impedance.

### ⚠️ The standard engine got this file wrong

`core/criteria.py` pools all 112 wire free sites in a file into one floor and takes the 1st
percentile. With two chips floating, their quiet steep inputs dragged the pooled threshold to a
spectral slope of −2.002, which is steeper than every animal channel, so the pooled run scored the
whole cohort at 0 to 13 % usable and would have condemned five good mice.

Rerunning with a **per chip floor**, where each animal is judged only against the fourteen bare
inputs on its own port and slot, gives the result above, and every chip passes its own
falsification check. **The pooled floor is unsafe the moment any one headstage floats, and it
fails silently**, because the falsification check asks whether the floor scores usable rather than
whether the chips agree with each other.

### Updated the Radiens reference page and fixed a wrong line on it

Answered a batch of questions about the Allego Signal Processing and Electrodes tabs and folded the
answers into [[radiens-software-reference]], which is the vault's primary page for that software.

🔴 **The important part is a correction.** That page said *"EEG = `EEG+ − EEG−` computed offline"*.
**That is wrong and it is the project's most expensive misreading**: the signals reaching the file
are already bipolar, differenced on the chip before the analog to digital converter, so `/1` IS the
electroencephalogram and `/2` IS the electromyogram and **nothing should ever be subtracted
offline**. A whole second analysis pipeline was built on that sentence and survived until
2026-08-10. Replaced with a danger callout.

Also added, all verified against our own recordings rather than assumed:

- **The three filter stages.** Hardware runs on the preamplifier chip before the converter and is
  **irreversible**; Data stage is software but still reaches the saved file; Visualization is
  display only.
- **What we actually have**, identical in every recording since 2026-07-14: a hardware band pass of
  **1.0977 to 475.17 Hz** on ports A to D, and **`software_stage_1` empty in every single file**, so
  no software filter has ever touched our recorded data.
- **Why the numbers look automatic.** The unpopulated ports E to H report exactly 1 and 475, the
  requested value, while the ports with real chips report 1.0977 and 475.17, which is what the Intan
  chip could actually realise from its registers. Allego is echoing an achieved value, not choosing
  a filter. The 1 Hz request came from the manual's own recommendation on p70.
- 🛑 **Never add a 60 Hz notch at any stage.** Our connection test measures 60 Hz against wire free
  inputs, and a per chip collapse in that 60 Hz is exactly how tonight's floating grounds were
  found. A notch erases that evidence silently.
- **Does the hardware filter matter given that we process offline from raw? Partly.** The 475 Hz top
  end is harmless anti alias work. The **1.0977 Hz bottom end is real and already paid for**: it
  irreversibly eats 0.5 to 1.1 Hz, which is why `BAND_DELTA` moved from 0.5 to 1.0 on 2026-08-08,
  and **4.7 to 16.1 % of the staging delta denominator still comes from that attenuated region**.
  Recommendation recorded: **leave it at 1 Hz** and state it as a methods limitation.
- **The Electrodes tab: we are already doing it right.** `hstg__passthrough_64` +
  `probe__default_8x8` on all four ports, and the manual says on p62 that passthrough is exactly the
  entry for a breakout board where site geometry is meaningless. A custom sensor map would buy us
  channel labels, but 🛑 **it changes `site_num`, and our whole analysis is written in site
  numbers**, so it would silently remap everything. Danger callout added there too.
