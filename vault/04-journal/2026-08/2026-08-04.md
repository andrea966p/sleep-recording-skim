**B1 and the remaining boards were then run and inspected too. Every board in the
fleet passes.** In particular **B1, the mirror case, did not fail on pads 2/3**,
which was the one result that could still have overturned this. The "the fault is
per pad rather than per channel type" hypothesis is therefore dead as well.

**Verdict, whole fleet: §5 row "all four pads identical, stable through rotation,
no shorts". The free tier is exhausted.** The fault is upstream of the slip-ring
socket, on every animal.

Three hypotheses die here:

- **"Cold solder joint on pad 0 or pad 1"** — dead. Every pad reaches its own pin
  with a clean beep, and pads 0/1 are indistinguishable from the proven-good EMG
  pads 2/3.
- **"The slip ring is the fault"** — dead, with the caveat below.
- **"The fault is per pad, not per channel type"** — dead. B1 was the test for
  this and it passed on pads 2/3.

#### Two artefacts seen, both benign, both worth recording so nobody re-chases them

1. **Capacitor charge transient mistaken for leakage.** Probing between nets gave
   numbers around 1100 to 1800, which then vanished to open. Values were **not
   repeatable** across re-probes (1100, 1400, 1354), there was **no beep**, and
   the same behaviour appeared on **every pad pair including the pad 2 to pad 3
   control**. That is the meter's test current charging the capacitance of the
   fan-out bundle and the board, exactly the artefact in §4.5 of the procedure. A
   real short is a steady near zero **with a beep that stays on**. Shorting the
   probes together before each pair dumps the charge and shrinks the transient.
2. **Vias beep.** Probing the small plated dots on the back of the adapter board,
   rather than the pads, produced clean beeps. Those are the traces surfacing
   through the board on the same net. Expected, not a defect, and useful for
   tracing where a net runs.

#### The honest limitation on the rotation result

The 360° rotation was done **by hand, unloaded, with the rig at rest**. The real
failure happens with an animal pulling on a tether for 27.9 hours. So this rules
out **gross** intermittency in the slip ring. It does **not** rule out
intermittency triggered by tension or vibration under load. Write it that way in
any report: the slip ring is not cleared, it is only cleared of the failure mode
a static bench test can see.

## ⭐⭐⭐ The electrodes are good — and a hypothesis proposed and disproved in one afternoon

⭐⭐⭐ **Full write-up: [[2026-08-04-movement-dependent-contact]]. Read that, not
this summary, before acting.** Sweep files, times and the session timeline:
[[impedance-sweep-register]].

After the DMM test I restarted everything, re-swept, and then broke the rig down
to one animal at a time with no splitter. Seven sweeps in 23 minutes, 16:33 to
16:56, all in `~/Downloads/impedance measurments/2026-08-04/`.

### The pair that started it

Animal **B1**, alone on **port B**, sweeps at **16:47** and **16:48**. Only
variable: whether the mouse was moving.

| B1, port B | EEG `B/1` | EMG `B/2` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **mouse STILL** | **0.052 MΩ @ 179.7°** | 2.099 @ 168.6° |
| **mouse MOVING** | **8.649 MΩ @ 54.6°** | 5.805 @ 33.2° |

**166× on the EEG channel, both channels moving together, phase swinging from
connected to open.**

**0.052 MΩ is the best impedance ever recorded on this rig**, EEG or EMG, by a
wide margin. B1's EEG electrode is not degraded. **It is excellent.** At the time I
concluded it disconnects when the animal moves; see the next section, where that
part did not hold up.

### THE ELECTRODES AND THE IMPLANTS ARE GOOD. THIS PART IS SOLID.

**D2 says it independently.** It was fully open after Run 2 at 9.200 MΩ, and today
it read connected on **both** channels in **every** sweep: 0.352/0.233 at 16:33,
0.459/0.270 at 16:40 alone, 0.400/0.088 at 16:56. The only animal to manage that,
and it streamed a clean signal. **Open channels recover completely.**

### Supporting single-animal sweeps

With one headstage and no splitter, an animal lands on `/1` and `/2` of its port,
**not** `/33` and `/34`.

- **D2 alone** (`2026-08-04/imp_2026-08-04_1640_D2-alone-portD-32ch_single-animal.csv`): `D/1` 0.459 @ 2.5°, `D/2` 0.270 @ 178.5°. D2 is

  **It never died.** (At the time I attributed the difference to movement; what it

- **B1 alone in port D** (`2026-08-04/imp_2026-08-04_1644_B1-alone-portD-32ch_port-swap-control.csv`): 0.873 and 0.325 MΩ. Works in both

### What every past observation now looks like

Every open finding is the same intermittency sampled at a different moment. Note
the third column: I originally wrote "an animal moving" in all of these, and the
replication below removed that. The correct reading is just "a different moment".

- Open electrode in **36 of 41** animal-recordings
- **Hum bimodal**, 9-90 µV vs 1000-4700 µV → one electrode, two contact states
- **522 contact flips in half a second** → the intermittency, directly observed

## ⚠️ The movement hypothesis failed its replication, same day

I ran a pre-registered test straight after: an 8 mouse sweep with **B1, B2 and C1
moving** and the other five still. File `2026-08-04/imp_2026-08-04_1656_8mice-64ch_replication-B1B2C1-moving.csv`.

**The prediction failed. Moved 3 of 6 connected, still 5 of 10 connected. 50%
against 50%, no effect.** B1 moving read **0.177 MΩ CONNECTED**, C1 moving read
connected on both channels, and D1, definitely still, read open on both.

**A sweep takes about 20 seconds**, so the animal's state held for the whole
measurement. My earlier caveat that "a moving sweep mixes postures" was wrong and
does not rescue the hypothesis.

### Reading the null honestly, in both directions

**The group comparison is weak evidence against.** It is unpaired: three animals
against five, each with its own baseline propensity, at a ~50% base rate. That
design has very little power, and its null does not refute much on its own. The
paired still-versus-moving design was the better one.

**What actually damages the hypothesis is B1 contradicting itself.** B1 read
8.649 MΩ moving at 16:48 and **0.177 MΩ connected while moving** at 16:56. Same
animal, same stated condition, opposite result, eight minutes apart. That is a
within-animal contradiction and no argument about pairing rescues it. **And
animals that were definitely still went open** (A2 both, D1 both), so the flipping
is not gated on gross movement at all.

**Status: plausible contributor, not established cause.** Settling it needs the
paired design run properly, alternating still and moving on one animal several
times.

### What survives

- ✅ **The contact is intermittent.** B1's EEG read **0.052, 8.649, 6.236 and
  0.177 MΩ in one afternoon.** Nothing about a fixed electrode condition can
  produce that.
- ✅ **The electrodes and implants are good.** 0.052 MΩ sits inside the published
  10 to 50 kΩ band. **No surgery is warranted on the current cohort.**
- ✅ **A single sweep cannot rank, screen or classify a channel.**
- ❌ **The trigger is unknown.** Not gross movement: animals that were definitely
  still went open.

Full detail and the replication table: [[2026-08-04-movement-dependent-contact]].

## Next tests, decided at the bench

1. **Direct connection, no slip ring.** Custom long tether from the animal
   straight to the adapter board and headstage, and watch it. Removes the largest
   untested mechanical element in one move.
2. **Rebuild the tether with soldered joints and resin** instead of thin
   heatshrink.
3. Open question raised: **implantation depth.** Whether the electrode pins need
   to sit deeper against the dura. Note this cannot help the current cohort and
   would be a protocol change for a future one.
4. Note on tether length: it is currently cut so that with the animal in the
   corner of the cage the tether runs perfectly straight to the slip ring, which
   may load the skull. **However, the animal that moved during the sweep was not
   at the edge and the tether was not under strong tension**, so tension does not
   explain that particular result.

> [!warning] Two traps in test 1, worth reading before building the tether
> **It changes two variables at once.** A new custom tether removes the slip ring
> *and* replaces the tether and its joints. If the problem disappears you will not
> know which change did it. Prefer bypassing the slip ring with the **existing**
> tether, or running the new tether through the existing slip ring first.
>
> **One clean sweep proves nothing.** At the current ~50% base rate, a direct
> connection reading good once is exactly what a coin flip looks like. **Compare
> flip rates over 20 minutes, not single readings.**
>
> And for test 2: soldered joints potted in resin are stiff, and stiff joints
> concentrate strain at the edge of the resin. Leave a deliberate flexible length
> between the resin and the headcap, or the break point may move rather than
> disappear.

## 📌 Tomorrow, 2026-08-05: the sweep loop

**Repeated impedance sweeps every ~30 s for ~20 minutes on one animal, timestamped**,
to measure the **flip rate** rather than a single state. Radiens has a Python API
