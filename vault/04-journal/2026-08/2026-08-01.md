## How I analysed it, and the one wrong turn

**The first attempt failed.** I wrote a continuous pass computing 1 s band power on every second of the run. It hit **0.6 % in 90 seconds**, implying about **4 hours**. Killed it.

The bottleneck is I/O, not CPU: `.xdat` is sample-interleaved, so reading any subset of columns still drags all 105 GB off disk, measured at roughly 630 MB/s.

**The fix was to sample instead of stream.** Pass 1 analyses a **10 s window every 60 s** — 1674 windows, about 1/6 of the file, a few minutes to run.

That trade is legitimate for the question being asked, and I want the limits written down:
- Duty cycle is an unbiased estimator under this sampling. The headline 78.7 % is sound.
- Segments lasting minutes to hours are resolved exactly.
- ⚠️ **Flip counts are LOWER BOUNDS.** Anything starting and ending inside the unobserved 50 s is invisible. "B1 frontal flipped 372 times" means **at least** 372.
- Sub-second transitions cannot be timed, which is why pass 2 re-reads a chosen window at full 1 kHz.

Two-pass — cheap survey, then targeted full-rate zoom — is the pattern to reuse on files this size.

**Measurement choices:** mains as the **55-65 Hz band**, never a single 60.00 Hz bin (mains drifts; single-bin understates 4-5×, an error I made earlier in this investigation). 4th-order Butterworth SOS with `sosfiltfilt` so zero phase distortion cannot smear transition times. Every 10 s window padded 5 s each side and the pad discarded, so filter transients never enter a number.

**I checked the OPEN threshold rather than asserting it.** 300 µV sits in the empty gap of the bimodal distribution. Sweeping it 150 → 600 µV moves the headline only 80.3 % → 76.8 %. But **five legs ARE threshold-sensitive** — B1/front, B2/EMG+, B2/EMG−, C2/front, D2/pariet — because they live near the boundary by nature. Those are exactly the chattering ones, and their duty cycles should not be quoted to better than about ±15 points. Everything else is threshold-independent: C1/front reads 68.8 / 68.8 / 68.7 % across the whole sweep.

**Controls ran the whole 27.9 h alongside the legs** — 16 internally tied floor pins, 2 per headstage. That is what turns "these numbers look odd" into "these numbers are odd and it is definitely not the instrument".

# ⭐ LATER THE SAME DAY — the channel map was wrong, and there is real sleep in the data

Andrea refused the EMG result: *"mice are done by professor, it's impossible that she did wrong all the emg, absolutely impossible. There must be something wrong we are doing on our end, I think we are looking at the data in a wrong way."*

That was correct, and it broke the whole thing open. Full write-up: [[2026-08-01-channel-mapping-correction-first-sleep]].

**The RHD2216 is BIPOLAR.** C3410 pads 0+1 are the two pins of ONE amplifier channel, and pads 2+3 are the two pins of the next one. So 4 pads make **2 channels, not 4**:

- `/1` = **EEG**, already bipolar → use directly
- `/2` = **EMG**, already bipolar → use directly
- `/3`, `/4` = **empty pads, no wire by design**

We had been computing `EEG = /1 − /2`, which is **EEG minus EMG**, and measuring the empty pads `/3`,`/4` and reporting them as **24 failed EMG surgeries**.

**Corrected results:** EMG works on **5 of 8 mice**. The real failure is the **EEG channel `/1`**, open on 8 of 8. **21.0 scoreable mouse-hours, not zero.**

⭐ **And there is real sleep architecture, replicated on 3 mice:**

| mouse | window | WAKE | NREM | REM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | 8.70 h | 66.8 % | 29.3 % | 3.9 % |
| D2 | 5.20 h | 80.8 % | 18.6 % | 0.6 % |
| C2 | 3.00 h | 37.9 % | 52.2 % | 9.9 % |

In every animal, independently: WAKE has the highest muscle tone, NREM the most delta and the biggest amplitude, REM the highest theta/delta and the lowest muscle tone. Sleep/wake persistence r = +0.70 to +0.80 at one minute against shuffle controls of −0.03 to +0.03.

**Also found:** `nperseg=512` at 1 kHz gives 1.95 Hz resolution, so the 6-9 Hz theta band held a single FFT bin and `np.trapz` returned exactly zero for every epoch. Fixed to `nperseg=2048`.

**The lesson worth keeping:** an implausible result is a bug report about your own method. "Every EMG failed on every mouse" should have triggered a channel-map audit immediately. The controls were right, the physics was right, the code ran correctly, and the conclusion was still wrong because the map was assumed rather than verified.
