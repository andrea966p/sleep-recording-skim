## Morning. Two surprises before lunch

I came in intending to run arm C of the tether comparison on D2. Two things changed the plan.

**A new mouse went on.** It was implanted on 21 July with the rest of the cohort but was left in
its home cage, never tethered, until this morning. That is **16 days of recovery** against the
8 to 10 days everyone else got, and it is the first animal in this project ever tethered in line
with our own ≥14 day protocol. It went on the 19.5 cm soldered tether from yesterday and it went
into the **B1 slot**, which was empty because the original B1 was sacrificed on 5 August.

**A2 turned out not to be an animal.** The A2 position holds a **cut tether attached to nothing**,
still wired through its slip ring, headstage and the port A splitter. There is no mouse. Nobody
planned this as an experiment; it just was.

## The teardown ladder, 12:04-12:27

Since A2 was a free control, I stripped it one element at a time to find where its false
"connected" readings come from. Files `02-` and `03-`.

| what was plugged in | reads below 2 MΩ |
| --- | --- |
| tether + slip ring + headstage | 22.5% |
| slip ring + headstage | 26.7% |
| **headstage alone** | **23.3%**, best 0.107 MΩ |

**Flat.** Removing the tether changed nothing; removing the slip ring changed nothing.

⚠️ The last rung, unplugging the headstage, is **degenerate** — it does not leave an empty
channel, it **removes the channels**. Port A dropped 64 → 32, total 256 → 224, and `A/33`
ceased to exist. Always `remap.py` after unplugging a headstage.

The real floor control was already in the data: **`/3`-`/16` and `/35`-`/48` are real amplifier
inputs with no wire on them**, 112 per sweep. They give 21.4% / 3.1%, matching the cut cable to
within one percent.

> ✅ **Our tethers, slip rings and splitters are not leaking. The false positives are the
> amplifier's own behaviour on a floating input.** ⚠️ The floor reached **0.051 MΩ**, the same as
> the best electrode value ever recorded here, so **a single low reading is never evidence.**

### The fair objection, and what it produced

Objection raised: that analysis judged **raw** data, so maybe good EEG hides under the hum and
appears after normal filtering. Correct objection, and testing it produced the most important
methodological result of the day.

I applied 60/120/180 Hz notch + 0.5-30 Hz band-pass, **and put the 112 wire-free channels through
the identical chain**.

> 🔴 **A channel with no wire attached comes out at a median 69 µV of smooth, plausible,
> EEG-looking signal.** Filtering manufactures a convincing trace out of nothing. Looking at
> cleaned traces cannot tell you whether a mouse is connected.

![[2026-08-06-traces-filtered.png]]

**Filtered traces**, 20 s each, **all at an identical ±400 µV scale** — that fixed shared scale is
what makes it honest, because auto-scaling would zoom into each channel's noise until every panel
looked beautiful. B1 is smooth and rolling with visible slow waves. Everything else, including the
no-mouse slot and the bare wires, is dense hash.

![[2026-08-06-psd.png]]

**Power spectrum**, log-log. B1 falls steeply from ~2 Hz across five orders of magnitude, the 1/f
shape of real neural activity. Every other line is a flat plateau, which is what noise is.
**Height is loudness; shape is biology.** Only B1 has the shape.

Quantified: floor slope range **[−1.10, −0.04]**; B1 **−2.15 with delta fraction 0.47**; B2 −0.47,
C2 −0.38, D2 −0.38, A2-no-mouse −0.04 — **all inside the bare-wire range**.

### Railing: can we just drop those epochs and keep the night?

Good proposal, now our standing policy in [[eeg-artefact-and-railing-rejection-policy]]. It does
not rescue today, for three reasons:

1. **Unit error.** "7% railed" was a fraction of **samples**. At **epoch** level the same channel
   is **77.6%**. Always state the unit.
2. **Not movement.** A1 and D1 rail in unbroken blocks of **91 and 130 minutes**, and the
   **no-mouse A2 slot rails 100% of the time**. There is no animal there to move. On this rig
   railing is the open-electrode signature.
3. **Rejection does not help.** Keeping only the perfectly clean epochs, the surviving spectral
   shape is still inside the bare-wire range for B2, C2, C1 and D2. Not spoiled EEG — not EEG.

**B1 had 0 railed epochs in 782.**

### Then it reversed, and the conclusion became provisional

At ~16:56, while twisting B1's slip ring by hand, one sweep gave **D2 EEG 0.396 and EMG 0.404** —
**below every one of the 40 arm C values**, whose minima were 6.992 and 0.694.

> 🔴 **Arm C may have captured one long open state rather than permanent harm.** Contact states on
> this rig persist for tens of minutes then flip, and 40 sweeps span only 21 minutes. The flat rho
> that excluded anaesthesia is **equally consistent with being stuck in one state**. The tether was
> also inspected and is **not twisted**, so binding is not the explanation either.
> **Do not report arm C harm as established.** The overnight recording settles it.

**Twisting B1's slip ring did no harm** — B1's EEG read **0.084 MΩ** during the manipulation, its
best value of the day. One sweep, so not a result, but no sign of damage.

⚠️ Still worth noting the direction: arm B (short soldered, **with** slip ring) 45→100%; arm C
(long soldered, **no** slip ring) 67→0%. That is the opposite of "the slip ring is the problem",
though it is confounded by animal and tether length.
