## Everything is archived: 130 files, 1.01 TB

The two new runs (31.2 GB + 149.7 GB = 182.3 GB) were copied to the NAS and verified. Independent
cross-check afterwards, not trusting the script's own report:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| File counts | local 130 = NAS 130 |
| Leftover `.part` files | 0 |
| Sum of file bytes | **1,013,471,371,569 on both sides, exact** |
| Per-file size diff across all 130 | **no differences** |
| Fresh sha256 of the 149.7 GB run vs the manifest | **match** |

> [!note] A 9,820-byte discrepancy that was NOT a problem
> `du -sb` initially showed the NAS as 9,820 bytes larger. That is the **directory inode itself**,
> which differs between ext4 and the NAS filesystem. Summing file sizes only, both sides are
> byte-identical. Worth remembering before anyone raises an alarm over it: compare files, not `du`.

Transfer ran at 60-70 MB/s. The slow part is not the network — it is the NAS computing sha256 over
149.7 GB, which took about an hour of the total.

### Two fixes to the archive while it was idle

- **`ARCHIVE_IGNORE_WINDOW=1`** lets a deliberate manual run bypass the 08:30-20:00 window. That
  window is only a crude proxy for "do not compete with a recording"; the recording check is the
  real protection and the override does **not** touch it.
- **The manifest lookup now happens before the rig query.** `recording_active()` spawns a python
  client and costs ~3.3 s, so asking it about already-archived files wasted about seven minutes on a
  resumed run. A full re-scan of 130 files went from ~7 minutes to **4.3 seconds**.

## Scored the 39.7 h run, and found a bug underneath it

Full report: [[2026-08-21-39h-run-score]].

**Three positions are fully scoreable: A1, A2, D2.** B2 is EEG only, B1 and D1 are dead, C1 and C2
pass the muscle test but are contradicted by independent physiology.

🔴🔴 **The wire-free floor was pooled across all eight amplifier chips when the project's own
definition says it must come from the same chip.** On this run that single choice is the entire
answer: pooled puts A1 at 41.5% usable with a 9.3 min block, per-chip puts it at **99.9% with a
457.5 min block**. Under the pooled floor **every** position fails rung 2.

✅✅ **The EMG gate passes — A1, A2 and D2** (initially it looked like four; see the correction below).
Until today this project had demonstrated state-dependent muscle tone on one channel ever, a control
at -0.734. **This is what was blocking the blind-scoring kappa.**

🔴 **Then an adversarial review proposed three falsification tests, and two animals died.** The
decisive one: **delta ratio carries EMG-band power in its denominator**, so muscle bleed into the
EEG channel would drive r negative in a dead animal. Recomputed against **absolute** delta power,
which has no denominator: A1 -0.580 → **-0.705**, A2 -0.525 → **-0.686**, D2 -0.485 → **-0.661** —
all three *strengthen*, the opposite of the artefact's prediction. But **C1 collapses from -0.733 to
-0.009** and **C2 reverses from -0.689 to +0.595**. Both are withdrawn.

⚠️ **C1 had the strongest number on the rig and it was an artefact.** Its inverted circadian EMG had
already flagged it; that warning was right. Two further tests: the per-chip criterion applied to the
112 wire-free inputs passes **0 of 112** (so it is not measuring chip self-similarity), but the
fixed **-0.30 bar is crossable by interference — 16 of 112 wire-free channels reach it** — so it must
be replaced by a per-chip null.

⭐ **The control that matters most:** re-running the 2026-08-08 gate per-chip moves exactly **one**
animal across the bar (B2, -0.156 → -0.366). The fix does not manufacture passes, so the four passes
here are a real change in the data.

⚠️ Correcting the record: the 08-11 audit's "no experimental animal passes the EMG gate" is **partly
wrong** — B2 does pass with the correct floor. Three of four still fail, so the wider claim stands.

🛑 **Still not a sleep result.** No hypnogram, no percentage asleep. Rung 4 stays PROVISIONAL until
the kappa exists.
