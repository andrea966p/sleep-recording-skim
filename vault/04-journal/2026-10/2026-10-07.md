### Case 3. Epoch 164, a microarousal
![[2026-10-07-scoring-04-e164-microarousal.png]]
- **Question:** "a semi-clear wake movement at the same time as the EEG has a brief decrease in amplitude. Is this a very fast
  brief awakening? How can this be defined?"
- **Seen:** one ~1.2 s burst (tone 14, under W's 27); the EEG smaller and faster for ~1 s; over the epoch δ 7k, γ 0.02, θ/δ 0.11.
- **Call:** NR, a **microarousal**. Majority rule: activity under half an epoch keeps the surrounding stage; half or more is W

### Case 7. Epoch 682, a similar burst that stayed NREM
![[2026-10-07-scoring-14-e682-dossier-burst-in-nrem.png]]
- **Question:** "682 is not marked as W, why? I think it's similar to 617."
- **Answer:** the rule's second check (`tone_islands` in `rule.py`). 682's EMG RMS (13.7) is above the wake cut, so it starts as W.
  But its **spike-proof tone is 8.0**, sleep level, because the burst (~1.3 s, plus ~0.5 s at the end of 681) fills only a third
  of the epoch. A short W run whose spike-proof tone stays under the cut is rewritten **NR** and marked `NR*` (reason `tone`). 617's
  burst filled most of its epoch, so it stayed W. σ over 28 s stays at 590: the spindles continue. Human scorers split on exactly
  these (the rule's notes: 13 % called W far below the cut, 63 % well above it).
- **Call:** NR, a microarousal.

### Concept questions
- **"What is spike-proof tone, and what are γ and σ markers of?"** The spike-proof tone is the median of the 0.5-s sliding EMG RMS:
  high only when the muscle is active for most of the epoch. **σ (10–15 Hz) = sleep spindles, NREM only**: the best check that the
  brain stayed in NREM during a movement. **γ (30–50 Hz) = activated cortex** (W and R), but muscle activity leaks into it.
  Details: [[sleep-scoring-handbook#2. The measures, explained|handbook §2]].
- **"Where do I see EMG RMS and spike-proof tone in the tool?"** EMG RMS is the `tone` number under the EMG trace, the
  `muscle tone` slider, the dossier's EMG row and the black curve of the EMG lane. The spike-proof tone was only in `_review.csv`
  (`emg_tone_med`) and as the floor of the lane's grey band, so it is **now a dossier row**.
- **Andrea's summary, corrected:** "EMG RMS is the EMG tone value, split into sub-second windows…". The **EMG RMS is one number over
  the whole 4 s**. The **spike-proof tone** is the one that splits the epoch into 0.5-s windows and takes the median.
