# Oral SOD1 in drinking water: sleep, activity, cognition and tissue oxidative stress in mice

> [!warning] 2026-11-03: superseded by [[2026-11-03-hlb-sod1-report-draft-v02]] (restructured, EEG re-score, intake, verified citations).

Draft v0.1, 2026-11-02, for review by Prof. Kim before any version goes to HLB.
Storyline and the reasons behind each choice: [[2026-11-02-hlb-final-report-storyline]].
Sections marked ⬜ wait for data; the numbers in them are placeholders, not results.

## Summary

**Question.** Does oral microbial SOD1 given in drinking water change sleep, activity or cognition in mice,
with normal sleep or after sleep deprivation, does it change oxidative-stress markers in tissue, and is
there any sign of harm?

**What we did.** Male C57BL/6J mice drank SOD1 (6.8 U/mL, about 20 U/day) or UV-sterilised water. Sleep
was measured with implanted activity loggers (nanotags) in two cohorts where SOD1 and water mice were
housed and tested together, and with EEG/EMG in four earlier cohorts, one per group, which also gave the
tissue. Behaviour was a Y-maze, open field and novel object recognition (NOR) battery under normal sleep,
and a Y-maze straight after sleep deprivation (SD).

**What we found.**
- **No sign of harm.** No toxicity or abnormal behaviour was seen, and the mice drank the SOD1 solution
  (⬜ intake and body weight to be analysed).
- **Sleep: no detectable change** in amount, light/dark distribution, fragmentation or daily rhythm, in
  either nanotag cohort. The smallest change these cohorts could detect is about 1-2 h/day.
- **Activity: SOD1 mice tended to be more active,** in three separate readouts and cohorts (Y-maze arm
  entries, open-field distance, dark-phase logger activity). None is conclusive alone.
- **Cognition with normal sleep: no detectable effect** on working memory (Y-maze) or recognition (NOR).
  Both groups performed above chance.
- **Tissue: no protection of the hippocampus.** Hippocampal SOD activity did not differ with SOD1, and
  hippocampal lipid peroxidation (MDA) was higher in the sleep-deprived cohorts whether or not they drank SOD1.
  Heart SOD activity showed a pattern that could reflect a peripheral SOD1 effect, but each group was a separate
  cohort, so it cannot yet be told apart from a cohort difference.
- ⬜ After SD: sleep rebound and Y-maze (cohort NS-SD, 11-03 to 11-04).

**Bottom line (provisional).** Oral SOD1 at this dose was well tolerated and did not measurably change
sleep, cognition or hippocampal oxidative-stress markers. These are small cohorts (4-9 mice per group), so
the study rules out large effects, not small ones. The heart result is the one lead worth testing properly.

## 1. Design, and what changed from the proposal

| | Proposal (2025-12) | Delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Route | gavage, 20 U/day | drinking water, 6.8 U/mL (about 20 U/day at about 3 mL/day), fresh every day |
| Groups | normal sleep or chronic sleep restriction, each ± SOD1 | the same four |
| n | 15 M + 15 F per group | 4-6 M per group per cohort |
| Sleep readout | EEG/EMG (NREM, REM, wake) | nanotag sleep/wake in the main cohorts; EEG/EMG in a small sub-study |
| Sleep loss | 4 h/day for 7 days | EEG cohorts: 4 h/day (09:00-13:00) for 7-8 days; NS-SD: 6 h/day (09:00-15:00) for 2 days |
| Behaviour | Y-maze, NOR (2 h retention) | Y-maze, open field, NOR (2 h) under normal sleep; Y-maze only after SD |
| Tissue | MDA, 8-isoprostane, SOD activity; cortex, hippocampus, liver, heart | SOD activity in heart and hippocampus, MDA in hippocampus (⬜ liver, NS cohorts) |

Why each change:
1. **Drinking water instead of gavage.** Daily gavage is a stressor that itself disturbs sleep and
   behaviour. The cost is that the dose depends on how much each mouse drinks, so intake is reported
   (section 3). SOD1 degrades in solution within about 30 h, so bottles were changed daily. How much
   activity is left in the bottle by the end of each day is not known and is outside what we can measure.
2. **Nanotags for most mice.** The EEG rig gave too few scoreable mice per cohort for group statistics.
   Nanotags give sleep amount, fragmentation, rhythm and activity in every mouse, but they infer sleep from
   immobility and give no NREM/REM split. The EEG mice are kept as a separate sub-study for sleep stages.
   Nanotag and EEG results are never pooled or used to confirm each other.
3. **SD by gentle handling at the start of the light phase (ZT0).** Mice sleep most in the early light
   phase, so SD there removes a large share of daily sleep, and gentle handling avoids the stress of
   automated devices. (⬜ citation to add.)
4. **NOR over 2 days with a 2 h retention interval,** and **only the Y-maze after SD.** Reasons in section 6.

## 2. Methods in brief
- **Mice.** Male C57BL/6J. EEG cohorts W-SD, S-SD, W-SL, S-SL (August to September 2026, one group each).
  Nanotag cohorts HLBNSL (SOD1 4, water 5; September to October) and NS-SD (SOD1 5, water 5; October to
  November). Lights 09:00-21:00.
- **Sleep.** Nanotag: immobility-defined sleep in 12 s bins, a 48-72 h baseline on water, then treatment.
  EEG/EMG: automatic staging checked by hand scoring (⬜ in progress).
- **Behaviour (HLBNSL).** Day 0 Y-maze (8 min); Day 1 open field (10 min, which is also NOR habituation);
  Day 2 NOR training, 2 h in the home cage, NOR test, then sampling. Tests from 15:00 (ZT6).
  Y-maze hand-scored; open field and NOR scored by pose tracking, NOR validated against blinded hand
  scoring by two raters.
- **Tissue.** Total SOD activity (Cayman 706002) in heart and hippocampus; MDA (Abcam ab118970) in
  hippocampus.
- **Statistics.** Section 8.

## 4. Sleep and activity (nanotag)
**HLBNSL, normal sleep, SOD1 4 vs water 5** ([[2026-10-05-HLBNSL-nanotag-merged-analysis]]).
- Total sleep on treatment: SOD1 793 ± 21 vs water 768 ± 39 min/day; SOD1 effect on the change from
  baseline +11 min/day (95 % CI −130 to +156). Same at every immobility cutoff (24-60 s).
- Fragmentation (bout number and length) and light/dark split: no detectable difference.
- Rhythm amplitude rose in SOD1 mice (p = 0.029), but SOD1 mice already differed at baseline and about
  35 tests were run. It did not repeat in NS-SD (below).

**NS-SD, before SD, SOD1 5 vs water 5** ([[2026-10-31-NS-SD-run1-nanotag-baseline-vs-sod1]]).
- First 6 days on treatment: SOD1 effect on total sleep +16 min/day (95 % CI about −18 to +58).
- Rhythm amplitude: no difference (p = 0.24), so the HLBNSL lead is not replicated.
- Dark-phase activity rose more in SOD1 mice (+616 vs +265 counts/h, p = 0.040), one of about 20 tests,
  with no change in sleep.

## 6. Behaviour

### 6.1 Normal sleep (HLBNSL, SOD1 4 vs water 5)
Details: [[2026-11-02-HLBNSL-behaviour-battery-summary]].

| Test | Measure | SOD1 | Water | SOD1 − water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y-maze | spontaneous alternation (%) | 60.5 ± 2.4 | 62.8 ± 2.4 | −2.2 (95 % CI −10.3 to +5.8) |
| Y-maze | arm entries | 48.3 | 38.4 | g +1.19, p = 0.063 |
| Open field | distance (m) | 63.9 | 51.1 | g +1.66, p = 0.11 |
| Open field | immobility (s) | 66 | 135 | g −1.59, p = 0.06 |
| Open field | centre time (%) | 18.6 | 15.9 | p = 0.73 |
| NOR | discrimination index d2 | +0.34 ± 0.13 | +0.37 ± 0.05 | p = 0.73 |

- **Working memory:** both groups alternate above chance (50 %); no detectable SOD1 effect.
- **Activity:** SOD1 mice entered more arms, moved more and stayed still less; anxiety-like measures do
  not differ.
- **Recognition:** the mice explored the novel object more (9/9 mice, p = 0.004, about two thirds of test
  time). In this cohort the novel object was always the same object at the same spot, so novelty cannot be
  fully separated from a preference for that object. No SOD1 difference in d2.

**Why NOR ran over 2 days with a 2 h retention.** The 2-day form is a standard variant with 1 day of
habituation (Leger et al. 2013), and our habituation session doubles as the open field. A 2 h interval tests
short-term recognition memory (Lueptow 2017), keeps training, test and sampling in one afternoon at a
matched time of day, and keeps the sleep after training out of the measurement. That matters because
sleep after training is what consolidates object memory: 6 h of SD right after training abolished
recognition 24 h later in mice, while the same SD 6 h later did not (Palchykova et al. 2006). The cost is
that a deficit only in overnight consolidation would be missed.

### 6.2 After sleep deprivation: Y-maze only (NS-SD) ⬜
Y-maze straight after the second 6 h SD (11-04, 15:00), then sampling.
SOD1 and water mice were tested alternately, 51, 56, 52, 57, 53, 58, 54, 59, 55, 60 (Andrea,
2026-11-05); start times and sequences: [[2026-11-04-NS-SD-ymaze-hand-scoring]]. Analysis ready 2026-11-04:
`ymaze_nssd.py` on the scoring CSV `NSSD_ymaze_hand_scoring.csv` (SA %, entries, against SD length) and
`ymaze_2x2.py` (normal sleep next to SD, SOD1 effect within each, SOD1 x sleep interaction, and each deprived
mouse's tag sleep against its alternation).

**Why only the Y-maze after SD.**
1. **It reads the state at the end of SD.** Spontaneous alternation is one 8 min session with no training
   and no retention interval (Kraeuter et al. 2019). NOR needs habituation, training, a retention interval
   and a test; with mice tested one after another, each would get a different amount of SD before training
   and a different chance to sleep after it.
2. **NOR memory depends on the sleep right after training** (Palchykova et al. 2006), and brief SD impairs
   hippocampal plasticity and memory (Vecsey et al. 2009). An SD design would make NOR results follow where
   SD fell relative to training, which differs between mice, and that confound could be larger than any
   SOD1 effect.
3. **Sleepy mice explore less.** More mice would fall below NOR's 20 s exploration minimum, and with 5 mice
   per group each exclusion is costly.
4. **Cleaner tissue.** Sampling follows the single test directly.
5. **Activity from the same trial.** Arm entries, distance and speed separate a memory change from an
   activity change, which matters because SOD1 mice tend to be more active.
6. **Comparable with the normal-sleep cohort,** which ran the same Y-maze. These are different cohorts, so a
   difference is consistent with an SD effect, not proof of one.
7. **Precedent:** Y-maze alternation is used to read SD-induced working-memory deficits and their rescue by an
   oral supplement in mice (Griffin et al. 2022).

## 7. Tissue: SOD activity and MDA (EEG cohorts)
Analysis and checks: [[2026-11-02-EEG-cohorts-tissue-SOD-MDA-reanalysis]].

**What was measured.** Total SOD activity (Cayman 706002; it measures Cu/Zn-, Mn- and Fe-SOD together, so it is
not specific to SOD1) in heart and hippocampus, and MDA, a lipid-peroxidation marker (Abcam ab118970), in
hippocampus. Tissue came from the four EEG cohorts: about 2 weeks on SOD1 or water, then 7-8 days of 4 h/day SD
or normal sleep. SD mice were sampled after about 22-24 h of recovery, so the SD groups show what remains a day
after the last SD bout. Values were recomputed from the raw absorbances with the kit's own calculation.

| Measure | Water, normal sleep | SOD1, normal sleep | Water, SD | SOD1, SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hippocampal SOD (U/mL) | 28.0 (9) | 25.8 (8) | 29.6 (9) | 30.7 (9) |
| Hippocampal MDA (nmol/well) | 1.47 (6) | 1.41 (6) | 1.69 (6) | 1.80 (5) |
| Heart SOD (U/mL) | 21.6 (6) | 27.2 (6) | 30.0 (6) | 21.7 (6) |

Mean (n). Two-way ANOVA (SOD1 × sleep).

- **Hippocampal SOD: no SOD1 effect** (p = 0.66). The SD groups were higher (p = 0.02), but they were assayed on
  a different plate from the normal-sleep groups, so this cannot be read as a sleep effect.
- **Hippocampal MDA: higher in the SD groups** (+15 % with water, +28 % with SOD1; p = 0.007) and **no SOD1
  effect** (p = 0.81). Oral SOD1 did not protect the hippocampus against the higher lipid peroxidation seen after SD.
- **Heart SOD: a crossover** (interaction p < 0.001). With water, the SD group was 15 % above normal sleep, which
  fits a compensatory rise in antioxidant defence. With SOD1, the normal-sleep group was already higher than water
  and the SD group was lower. The result holds with different curve fits but depends on single mice. Buffer volume
  was fixed per mg of tissue, so activity per mL of homogenate is also activity per mg.

**How to read it.**
- **Hippocampus.** No effect of SOD1 is the expected result. Plain oral SOD is largely digested: unprotected melon
  SOD left blood and liver enzymes unchanged in mice, while a gliadin-combined form raised them (Vouldoukis et al.
  2004). Native SOD also crosses the blood-brain barrier poorly. A direct effect in the brain was therefore unlikely,
  and any effect would have to be indirect, through the gut or the circulation.
- **The MDA rise after SD needs care.** Gentle-handling SD often leaves brain lipid peroxidation unchanged in
  rodents (Gopalakrishnan et al. 2004; review Villafuerte et al. 2015), and here it persisted after a day of
  recovery. The SD and normal-sleep groups were also separate cohorts, and one SD cohort had heavier hippocampal
  samples (16 vs 8-10 mg). We report it as a difference between the SD and normal-sleep cohorts, consistent
  with SD, that needs confirming within one cohort.
- **Heart.** A higher heart SOD with SOD1 under normal sleep matches oral melon SOD raising cardiac SOD by 30-40 %
  in rats (Carillon et al. 2014). The lower value with SOD1 after SD has two readings that these data cannot
  separate: less oxidative load, so less need for the compensatory rise, or a blunted adaptive response, as
  antioxidants do after exercise (Gomez-Cabrera et al. 2008; Ristow et al. 2009). A heart damage marker (heart MDA)
  would separate them. Above all, the four groups are four cohorts, and a cohort difference produces exactly this
  kind of pattern.
- **Safety.** Nothing in the tissue points to harm: SOD1 did not raise MDA in the hippocampus and did not lower
  SOD activity.

## 8. Statistics
**The experimental unit and what it limits.** In the nanotag cohorts, SOD1 and water mice were housed, handled and
tested together, so SOD1 vs water is a fair comparison within each cohort. In the EEG cohorts each group was its own
cohort (surgery, housing and sampling dates differ), so every group comparison there, including the tissue, is also
a comparison between cohorts. Mouse-level p values for the EEG-cohort tissue describe cohort differences, not causal
effects (Lazic et al. 2018).

| Endpoint | Lead analysis | Support | Reported as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nanotag sleep and activity | Difference between groups in each mouse's change from baseline, with 95 % CI | Exact permutation test | 1-2 primary outcomes (total sleep, dark-phase sleep); the rest exploratory |
| Behaviour, normal sleep | Group difference with 95 % CI; NOR d2 against 0 | Exact Mann-Whitney / permutation | Secondary |
| Y-maze after SD vs normal sleep | Side-by-side description | none | Different cohorts, no pooled test |
| Tissue | Group means with 95 % CI | Two-way ANOVA (SOD1 × sleep) with Šidák, as in the proposal | Exploratory, labelled as cohort-confounded |
| EEG sleep stages | Per-mouse values and hypnograms | none | Cases |

Why:
- **Estimation first.** With 4-9 mice per group, the size of a difference and its confidence interval say more
  than a p value (Ho et al. 2019). Exact permutation tests make no normality assumption and are valid at small n
  (Ludbrook 1994).
- **The proposal's two-way repeated-measures ANOVA** fits where both factors are crossed inside one cohort, the
  nanotag group × period design. There, with two periods, its interaction is the same contrast as the
  change-from-baseline comparison above, which the permutation test tests exactly. Tukey adds nothing in a 2 × 2.
- **Multiplicity.** About 20 nanotag outcomes were computed per cohort. With 4 vs 5 mice the smallest possible
  exact p is 0.016, so no correction across 20 outcomes can ever be passed. We name the primary outcomes and treat
  everything else as exploratory and descriptive, as ARRIVE 2.0 asks (Percie du Sert et al. 2020).
- **Non-significant results** are written as "no detectable difference; the data are compatible with effects from
  [CI]", never "no effect". At 80 % power and α = 0.05, these cohorts detect only large effects: Cohen's d about
  2.2 (4 vs 5), 2.0 (5 vs 5), 1.8 (6 vs 6), 1.4 (9 vs 9). Significant results at these n also tend to overstate
  the true effect (Button et al. 2013).
- **Outliers** are reported with and without, never removed without an independent reason (Ludbrook 2008).

## 9. Interpretation
1. **No harm.** The mice drank the SOD1 solution, and no toxicity, abnormal behaviour or sign of oxidative damage
   from SOD1 was seen.
2. **No detectable effect on sleep** in amount, distribution, fragmentation or rhythm, in two nanotag cohorts. Only
   large effects (about 2 h/day in HLBNSL, about 1 h/day in NS-SD) can be ruled out.
3. **No detectable effect on cognition with normal sleep.**
4. **A mild activity increase is the one consistent behavioural signal,** seen in three readouts across two
   cohorts with sleep unchanged. Each alone is inconclusive. We report it as a trend to test, not a finding.
5. **No protection of the hippocampus.** Consistent with the enzyme being digested and not reaching the brain.
6. **The heart pattern is the one tissue lead,** and the only sign that SOD1 may act outside the gut. It cannot be
   separated from cohort differences in this design.
7. ⬜ After SD: sleep rebound and Y-maze (NS-SD).

Why a null may also reflect delivery: the dose depends on how much each mouse drinks (⬜ section 3), the enzyme
degrades in solution over the day, and plain oral SOD is largely digested. A product protected for oral delivery,
or measured activity in the bottle over 24 h, would be needed to rule this out. That is outside this study.

## 10. Limitations
- **Small cohorts** (4-9 mice per group): only large effects are detectable.
- **Males only**, against the proposal's males and females.
- **Cohort confounding** in the EEG cohorts and their tissue: one cohort per group.
- **Immobility-based sleep** (nanotag): no NREM/REM, and quiet wake counts as sleep.
- **Dose uncertainty:** intake-dependent dose, unknown activity left in the bottle, coating and isoform of the
  powder unknown.
- **Tissue:** total SOD, not SOD1; no protein normalisation; hippocampal SOD plates split by sleep condition;
  SD tissue taken after a day of recovery; MDA by the TBA reaction is not fully specific for MDA.
- **NOR:** the novel object was always the same object at the same spot, so novelty is not fully separated from
  object preference; a 2 h test does not cover overnight consolidation.
- **SD protocols differ** between the EEG cohorts (4 h/day for 7-8 days) and NS-SD (6 h/day for 2 days).

## 11. What a follow-up should change
1. **Keep SOD1 and water, and SD and normal sleep, inside one cohort,** randomised by cage and surgery day.
2. **Run the tissue assays on the nanotag cohorts first** (HLBNSL, NS-SD), where SOD1 and water mice share a
   cohort: heart SOD, heart MDA, hippocampal MDA, on one plate with samples interleaved and coded blind, with
   protein normalisation. This is the direct test of the heart lead and costs no new mice: the tissue of both
   cohorts is stored at −80 °C.
3. **Size the next study for a realistic effect:** about 17 mice per group for d = 1.0, about 9 for d = 1.5.
4. **Pre-register** the primary sleep outcome, the primary tissue marker and the analysis.
5. **Counterbalance the NOR objects and sides,** and add a 24 h retention group if consolidation matters.
6. **Ask HLB** whether the powder is coated and which SOD it is (Cu/Zn or Mn), whether a protected oral
   formulation exists, and for the enzyme's stability in water.
