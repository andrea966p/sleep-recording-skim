## Why D2 had to go

D2's WAKE epochs have HIGHER EEG amplitude than its NREM (75.9 vs 72.3 µV) and essentially the SAME delta (41.6 vs 42.1 %), and its REM has HIGHER muscle tone than NREM (27.4 vs 12.4 µV). Physiologically wrong.

Yesterday it passed because I scored it on a hand-picked window using C1's hand-picked 25 µV EMG threshold. Under a per-animal Otsu threshold plus an amplitude gate, it fails. **That is the gate doing its job, and it is a lesson about reusing a constant derived from one animal on another.**

C2 passes but is not strong either: its NREM/WAKE delta separation is only 19.6 % vs 17.9 %. **C1 remains the one solid case.**

**A1 and D1 have perfect EMG for the full 27.9 h and no EEG at all. B1 has excellent EEG and no EMG.** The two failures are complementary, and on those three animals fixing either side roughly doubles scoreable time.

EEG failures are almost entirely **OPEN**, not shorted — shorted totals just 4.3 h across all 8 mice. So it is disconnection, not a short.

## Method hardening applied

1. **Three-part gate**: not open AND not shorted (EEG rms ≥ 25 µV) AND not clipped. Hum alone let shorted windows through on 2026-08-01.
2. **Otsu threshold per animal** instead of the hand-picked 25 µV the council flagged as arbitrary.
3. **Structure verified BEFORE any hypnogram is reported.** Below a persistence floor the animal is reported as "NO STRUCTURE" rather than given percentages — a dead channel will always produce some split if you insist on asking for one.
4. **Automatic physiology PASS/FAIL** per animal. This is what caught D2.

# Later — impedance verdict REVERSED, and run 2 started

## The impedance data was working all along

Andrea asked whether I had re-read the impedance with the corrected channel map. I had not. Doing so **reverses my 2026-07-31 verdict** that impedance snapshots are not a go/no-go gate.

Read on the REAL channels only (`/1`=EEG, `/2`=EMG, ignoring the empty pads `/3`,`/4`), against hours-of-usable-data from the 27.9 h run:

**Spearman ρ = −0.941, p = 5.7e-08** — from a sweep taken about two hours BEFORE that recording started.

| threshold | accuracy |
|---|---|
| Z < 0.9 MΩ | 13/16 |
| **Z < 2.0 MΩ** | **14/16** |

The three misses are marginal cases: B2's EMG (1.4 h of 27.9) and C2's EEG (4.2 h of 27.9) were mostly open, so the snapshot had the dominant state right. Only B1's EEG at 1.717 MΩ is a genuine threshold miss.

**Why it looked useless before:** half the "electrodes" being scored were bare pads. Include `/3` and `/4` in a summary and the signal drowns.

**And the reproducibility complaint was overstated.** Values wobble severalfold but the CLASSIFICATION is stable:

```
B1 EEG   0.222 -> 0.623  (2.8x)   CONN -> CONN   STABLE
B1 EMG   0.329 -> 0.026  (0.08x)  CONN -> CONN   STABLE
pad /3   5.380 -> 3.626           OPEN -> OPEN   STABLE
pad /4   8.140 -> 4.887           OPEN -> OPEN   STABLE
```

Even the infamous **102× swing was real information**: that channel was B2's EMG, which worked only 1.4 h out of 27.9 in the recording. The sweep was correctly flagging a flaky channel and I misread it as instrument noise. New rule: **classify, do not quote the number** — and a channel whose CLASS flips between back-to-back sweeps is genuinely intermittent, which is itself the finding.

**A third independent confirmation of the bipolar map:** empty pads `/3,/4` median 5.902 MΩ vs no-wire control 5.557 MΩ. The impedance data had been saying those pads had no wire on them the whole time.
