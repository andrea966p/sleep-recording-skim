## Why D2 had to go

D2's WAKE epochs have HIGHER EEG amplitude than its NREM (75.9 vs 72.3 µV) and essentially the SAME delta (41.6 vs 42.1 %), and its REM has HIGHER muscle tone than NREM (27.4 vs 12.4 µV). Physiologically wrong.

Yesterday it passed because I scored it on a hand-picked window using C1's hand-picked 25 µV EMG threshold. Under a per-animal Otsu threshold plus an amplitude gate, it fails. **That is the gate doing its job, and it is a lesson about reusing a constant derived from one animal on another.**

C2 passes but is not strong either: its NREM/WAKE delta separation is only 19.6 % vs 17.9 %. **C1 remains the one solid case.**

**A1 and D1 have perfect EMG for the full 27.9 h and no EEG at all. B1 has excellent EEG and no EMG.** The two failures are complementary, and on those three animals fixing either side roughly doubles scoreable time.

EEG failures are almost entirely **OPEN**, not shorted — shorted totals just 4.3 h across all 8 mice. So it is disconnection, not a short.

## Method hardening applied

1. **Three-part gate**: not open AND not shorted (EEG rms ≥ 25 µV) AND not clipped. Hum alone let shorted windows through on 2026-08-01.
2. **Otsu threshold per animal** instead of the hand-picked 25 µV the council flagged as arbitrary.
3. **Structure verified BEFORE any hypnogram is reported.** Below a persistence floor the animal is reported as "NO STRUCTURE" rather than given percentages — a dead channel will always produce some split if you insist on asking for one.
4. **Automatic physiology PASS/FAIL** per animal. This is what caught D2.
