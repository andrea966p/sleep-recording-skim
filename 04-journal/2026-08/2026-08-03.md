# 2026-08-03 — Second scoring bug found; every recording rescored; C1 survives, C2 joins it

Started as "re-examine the old recordings now that the channel map is fixed — we might have had good mice before". Ended with a second analysis bug found, all 8 substantive recordings rescored, and a full data-quality report written for the PI.

## Headlines

- 🔴 **Second bug found: the persistence statistic measured correlation ACROSS TIME GAPS.** `r@1min` was computed on the array of epochs that passed QC, which is not continuous in time. With intermittent contact that array is a concatenation of fragments, so index *k* and *k+15* can be hours apart.
- ⚠️ **It failed in BOTH directions.** Run 2's C2 scored `+0.078` ("NO STRUCTURE") when the test was *impossible to pass*; 07-13's D2 scored **`+0.854`**, the best number in project history, on a channel whose longest continuous stretch is **39 minutes of 100% WAKE**.
- ✅ **Fixed and calibrated.** Persistence is now computed strictly WITHIN unbroken blocks. Validated on synthetic data: pure noise → −0.035, 4-min bouts → +0.510, 10-min bouts → +0.703.
- ✅ **C1 SURVIVES the corrected metric and is stronger than we thought**: 8.73 h, a single **8.1-hour unbroken block**, `r@1min +0.744`, `@5min +0.583`, `@10min +0.435`, physiology PASS.
- 🆕 **C2 (same run) now also qualifies** — 1.69 h in testable blocks, `r@1min +0.532`, physiology PASS. Genuine n=2 replication.
- ✅ **D2 correctly REJECTED** (r=0.250 *and* physiology FAIL), independently confirming the 2026-08-02 withdrawal.
- 🔴 **Six "recoveries" from the old recordings were FALSE and are discarded** — including apparent sleep in the 72-hour run.
- 📄 **Report for the professor written**: [[2026-08-03-data-quality-report-for-professor]].

## Impedance: predicts CLASS, not YIELD

Run 2 pre-registration scored **4 HIT / 3 MISS / 1 VOID** ([[2026-08-02-run2-preregistration]] now filled in).

# Part 2 — light schedule recorded, thresholds re-derived, pipeline built

The professor's feedback on the report: the cross-recording meta-analysis was not
what was needed (different mice, different conditions, mixing them is not
meaningful). What is needed is **certainty that the data is clean** and **a
defined flow from raw recording to sleep features**. Also: verify the discard
thresholds against the literature, do not guess.

## 🔑 Light schedule finally recorded

**Lights ON 09:00, OFF 21:00 KST. 12:12 LD. ZT0 = 09:00 KST.** Written into
[[production-values]] and into `qc_thresholds.py`. Blank since 2026-08-01.

This unlocked the single most valuable check we have, and it is **completely
independent of every threshold we chose**.

## ⭐ C1 validated against circadian biology

| animal | light phase | dark phase | asleep in light | literature (C57BL/6) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **C1** | 33.1% wake | 82.2% wake | **66.9%** | ~70% asleep, NREM 62.0 ± 7.8 %/12 h |
| C2 | 47.5% wake | 99.3% wake | 52.5% | consistent, weaker |
| D2 | 59.0% wake | 83.9% wake | 41.0% | **outside** normal range |
| 06-29 D1 | 95.1% wake | no data | **4.9%** | **wildly outside** |

**C1 lands inside one standard deviation of the published mouse norm**, and the
light/dark contrast runs the right way (asleep in the light, active in the dark).
Nothing in our gate or our persistence statistic knows about the clock, so this
is an external check. Three independent lines — persistence, physiology,
circadian — now rank the animals identically: C1 strong, C2 real, D2 fails.

**06-29/D1 is now explained.** That recording is entirely in the LIGHT phase
(ZT9.1–ZT13.6), when a mouse should be ~70% asleep. It was 95% awake on
technically flawless data. That is not an artefact, it is an animal that was not
sleeping — consistent with first-tethering acclimatisation.

## 🔴 Thresholds were guesses. Re-derived from 1,137,955 epochs.

Audited every constant against the pooled data instead of trusting it.

| constant | was | now | basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RAIL` | 6300 µV (guess) | **6389.6 µV** | `0.195 µV/LSB × 32767`. MEASURED: max\|sample\| is 6389.8 µV on all 8 channels = 16-bit ADC full scale |
| `OPEN` | 300 µV | **150 µV** | mains is BIMODAL in log space: connected 84 µV, open 3731 µV, **valley 134 µV**. 300 sat on the OPEN flank = permissive |
| `SHORT` | 25 µV | **split** | → `FLAT = 11 µV` (epoch) + `DEAD_CHANNEL = 20 µV` (channel median) |
| `CLIP_FRAC` | 1e-3 | **0** | free — results identical for 0, 1e-3, 1e-2 |

**The old 25 µV rule was the single load-bearing constant** and it was wrong: it
discarded **9.5% of ALL epochs**, including real low-amplitude wake. At 13 µV an
extra animal passed; at 40 µV C1's `r` fell 0.74 → 0.43. A short is a property of
the **channel**, not of an epoch, so it now gets a statistic computed over the
whole recording — which removes the sensitivity entirely.

### ✅ Sensitivity swept: conclusions do NOT depend on the thresholds

- **C1 invariant** — `r = 0.74`, 8.7 h, in **every** variant tested.
- **C2 survives every variant** (`r` 0.31–0.72).
- Clipping is not load-bearing at all.

### Impedance, against the literature

Epidural screws in rodents are **5–50 kΩ at 1 kHz**; ≤10 kΩ confirms contact.
**Our best channel in the entire fleet is 73 kΩ.** So our 2 MΩ "connected" bar is
~40× looser than the norm — it is an **open-circuit detector, not a quality bar**.
A channel at 0.5–2 MΩ is a poor electrode that merely is not fully open.

## 🔴 REM is not reliably separable — stop reporting it

The 80th-percentile rule forces REM to 20% of sleep. An independent Otsu cut on
theta/delta gives 24–48% of sleep. **The literature value for mice is <10%.**
Both rules land far above it, so neither is a measurement. Report **WAKE vs
NREM** and REM's **order** (entered from NREM). Do not report a REM share.

### Two parser bugs found while wiring impedance into the pipeline

1. **The ohm sign has two Unicode code points** (U+03A9 GREEK CAPITAL OMEGA and

   completely wrong conclusion delivered silently. Columns are now located by
   **prefix**, never by exact header text. Same class as the earlier shell

# Part 3 — the two 8-mouse runs, compared directly

Narrowed the focus to the only two full 8-mouse runs, which is what the science
actually rests on. Written up as [[2026-08-03-two-8-mouse-runs]].

## Run 2 is a total loss

**8/8 OPEN, every EEG channel open for 100% of 21.7 h.** C2 flickered at 1.95%
of epochs, no block long enough to test. Run 1 is the only 8-mouse run that ever
produced anything: C1 8.73 h, C2 2.34 h scoreable, D2 2.76 h usable but rejected
at `r = 0.25`.

## 🔴 The degradation is directional and EEG-specific

Across Run 2 (pre-run sweep 08-02 01:44 → post-run 23:34):

**EEG: 8 worse, 0 better. EMG: 3 better, 3 unchanged, 2 worse.**

Same tether, slip ring, headstages, board, session, animals. A shared-path or
environmental fault cannot degrade one channel type and spare the other. This is
the cleanest evidence yet that the fault sits on the EEG side of the connection.

**C1's EEG died between the runs and never came back:**
`0.465 → 5.720 → 7.929 MΩ`. One-way, no chattering back. The best animal in the
project lost its EEG in the ~4 h gap and stayed lost.

## The impedance screen called Run 2 wrong

**B1 read 0.133 MΩ before Run 2 — best EEG impedance in the fleet — then ran at
2802 µV hum, open, for 21.7 h.** Resting impedance is not contact under load.
Narrowed [[electrode-connection-state-test]] accordingly: the ρ = −0.941 yield
claim did not replicate (Run 2 gave ρ = −0.452, pre-registration 4 HIT/3 MISS).

## Pattern, not yet a finding

The two mice that carried EEG in Run 1 (C1, D2) are the only two whose EEG
impedance rose across the gap; all five that carried nothing improved.
**ρ = +0.464, p = 0.25, n = 8.** C2 counters it. Hypothesis: *carrying signal is
what wears the contact out*. Consequential if true — track it next run, do not
act on it yet.
