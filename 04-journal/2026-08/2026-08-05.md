## Plan for today: the tether comparison

Full design: [[2026-08-05-tether-comparison]]. Yesterday: [[2026-08-04]].

Two new tethers built, **both soldered joints under resin** instead of thin heatshrink:
1. **short standard**, still through the slip ring
2. **long custom**, plugs onto the mouse and goes **straight to the adapter board, no slip ring**

Because both are built the same way, **arm C against arm B is a clean single variable
test of the slip ring**. That is why the long tether goes last.

### Decisions taken before starting

- **Manual sweeps.** No loop script today. Caps sample size at 20 per arm, which caps power.
- **No cameras.** Pis and cameras stay removed until further notice ⇒ **no movement covariate**,
  so today says nothing about the movement hypothesis and must not try to.
- **B1 is the test animal.** Only animal with a proven good electrode (0.052 MΩ), maximum
  volatility (166× in 23 min), baseline duty cycle near 50%, and port already discharged
  as a confound.
- **D2 stays plugged into port D, untouched, all session.** A sweep reads every port at
  once, so this is a free concurrent control. **Do not swap its tether.**
- **D1 is the backup** if B1's baseline comes out 0/20 or 20/20.
- A1 EMG, C1 EMG, C2 EMG rejected: connected in all four historical sweeps ⇒ at the
  **ceiling**, cannot show improvement.

### ⭐ Pre-registered predictions, written BEFORE arm A

| if the fault is | then |
| --- | --- |
| tether joints | B ≫ A, C ≈ B |
| the slip ring | C ≫ B, B ≈ A |
| both | A < B < C, monotone |
| neither, ie headcap or implant interface | all three ≈ 50%, D2 solid throughout |
| measurement or room | D2 churns too, no arm comparison means anything |

Row four is a likely outcome and **is not a failure**. It would clear the tether and the
slip ring by measurement rather than by assumption.

Primary outcome is **duty cycle**, the fraction of 20 sweeps reading \|Z\| < 2 MΩ. At a
~50% flip rate a single reading is a coin toss and cannot be used for anything.

**Power, accepted in advance:** 20 sweeps per arm detects 50% → 90% (p ≈ 0.008) but **not**
50% → 80% (p ≈ 0.10). An arm landing in that middle band gets **extended**, not argued about.

## ⭐ Sweeps are now AUTOMATED. The plan's "manual only" constraint is gone.

Found during setup: **Allego runs on this machine** (`e10ubuntu`), not on a separate PC.
`allegoserver` exposes gRPC on `50051`, and the impedance measurement is reachable.

**How it works, so this is repeatable:**

| piece | detail |
| --- | --- |
| RPC | `AllegoCore.GetIntanImpedance(ImpedanceRequest{port})` → `{ntvChanIdx, zMag, zPhase}` |
| protos | `/opt/allego/resources/radix/*.proto`, already compiled inside the `radiens` package |
| auth | **Cognito Bearer token**, not the device UUID. Reuse `radiens.utils.interceptors.SessionMetaData._instance._id_token` after one `AllegoClient().get_status()` |
| library | `radiens` v4.0.0 in `/opt/neurosurveillance/venv` |
| runner | `sweep_loop.py`, writes one Allego-format CSV per sweep plus a `_arm*_log.tsv` |

> [!warning] Three traps, all of which would have failed silently
> 1. **`generate_hardware_uuid()` prints "Unsupported OS: Linux" and returns nothing.** The
>    deviceUUID path cannot authenticate here at all. Only the Bearer token works.
> 2. **The `port` argument appears to be ignored.** Asking for port B returns **all 64**
>    channels of both connected ports. Do not assume the reply is filtered.
> 3. **`ntvChanIdx` is global, not per port.** 0-31 = `B/1..B/32`, 32-63 = `D/1..D/32`.
>    Verified twice, independently: the channel metadata table names them `port_B_0 …
>    port_D_63`, and fingerprint correlation against the 16:26 GUI export gives
>    **+0.697 for identity against +0.402 for the shifted alternative**. Getting this
>    backwards would have swapped B1 and D2 in every single file.

> [!important] `GetIntanImpedance` really does fire a fresh measurement
> This was the thing that had to be checked before automating anything, because a cached
> value would have produced 20 identical files and a completely fake duty cycle. Two calls
> three seconds apart returned **different values**, and each call takes **~30 s**. It
> measures.

**Consequence for the design:** cadence is 60 s and sample size is no longer limited by
how long someone is willing to press a button. The power ceiling written into the plan
(20 sweeps per arm detects only 50% → 90%) can be lifted by simply running longer arms.

`radiens` has **no impedance method in its public API** at all, hence the raw gRPC call.

> [!success] The control did its job
> `D/2` was connected in **20 of 20** sweeps with **zero** flips and a median of 0.269 MΩ.
> The measurement is clean, the room is not doing anything, and B1's churn is real rather
> than an artefact of sweeping repeatedly. **`D/2` also hit 0.017 MΩ**, a new best for this
> rig, comfortably inside the published 10 to 50 kΩ band.

> [!danger] ⭐ The most important result of arm A is inside D2, not B1
> **One animal, one tether, one slip ring, one connector, and its two channels behave
> completely differently: `D/2` 100%, `D/1` 40% with 8 flips.**
>
> Whatever flips the contact is therefore **per channel**, not common to the animal. That
> rules out every *shared* mechanism in one stroke: the ground, the connector unseating,
> whole-tether tension, the animal's posture as a global state.
>
> ⚠️ It does **not** exonerate the tether or the slip ring, and must not be reported as
> doing so. Both carry **separate conductors and separate rings per channel**, so a single
> bad joint or a single dirty ring is per channel too, and remains fully compatible with
> this result. Arms B and C are still the right tests.

> [!warning] Retraction, same session
> Partway through arm A, at 5 sweeps, I flagged that `B/1` and `B/2` looked like they were
> moving together and suggested a shared path. **The full 20 sweeps do not support that.**
> They share a state in **13 of 20** sweeps, which is barely above the 50% expected by
> chance at these rates. The apparent coupling was a 5 sample illusion. Discard it.

### ⭐⭐ Behaviour was logged by eye during arm A, and it settles one thing

Operator log, B1: movement at 16:44:15, 16:45:00, 16:45:47, 16:46:32, grooming from
16:47:37, to water 16:50:40, moving 16:51:00, climbed the cage edge 16:51:40, eating
16:53:20. **D2 slept through the entire block**, moving its head only at 16:44:30 and
16:45:30, both of which fall outside any sweep window.

> [!success] ⭐ MOVEMENT IS NOT NECESSARY FOR THE CONTACT TO FLIP. This is solid.
> **`D/1` flipped 8 times in 20 minutes in a completely still, sleeping animal**:
> `oooooCooCCCoooCCoCCo`. No handling, no locomotion, nothing touched.
>
> Any explanation that requires the animal to move is now dead for at least this channel.
> Whatever drives the flip operates in a sleeping mouse.

> [!note] And the sign is backwards, three times running, none of them significant
> Sweeps whose measurement window contains a logged movement event, against the rest:
>
> | channel | during logged movement | rest of block |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | `B/1` | **3/4 = 75%** | 6/16 = 38% |
> | `B/2` | 0/4 = 0% | 4/16 = 25% |
>
> `B/1`'s four best readings of the whole block (0.178, 0.345, 0.395, and 0.774 during
> grooming) all fall during or beside logged activity. Fisher exact on 3/4 against 6/16
> gives **p ≈ 0.28**, so this is **not significant** and n = 4 is tiny.
>
> What makes it worth recording is that it is the **third independent observation with the
> same sign**: 2026-08-04 B1 read 0.177 connected while moving, the 16:26 config check had
> B1 asleep and open while D2 was moving and connected, and now this. Each is weak. None
> supports "movement breaks the contact", and all three lean the other way.

> [!warning] Only about half of wall-clock time is actually measured
> A sweep occupies ~30 s of each 60 s cadence, so events at 16:45:47, 16:46:32, 16:47:37
> and 16:51:40 fell in the **gaps between sweeps** and cannot be scored at all. Four of the
> nine logged events were unusable for this reason. If behaviour is ever the primary
> question, drop the cadence to back-to-back sweeps and accept that the animal's state must
> hold for 30 s.

> [!warning] Phase is not a reliable second classifier
> Magnitude and phase disagree often: `D/1` read 2.245 MΩ at 167.8° (open by magnitude,
> capacitive by phase) and `B/1` read 2.612 MΩ at 14.2°. Meanwhile plenty of solidly
> connected readings sit near 2°, not 180°. The clean "180° connected against 30-55° open"
> split from single sweeps does **not** survive contact with 20 sweeps. Use magnitude as
> the classifier and treat phase as descriptive only, until someone works out what the two
> low phase populations actually are.

## Interlude 17:12-17:25: the unipolar LFP mouse on port A

Different surgery, depth LFP, unipolar headstage on the head, SPI straight to XDAQ, no
tether, no slip ring. Plugged in for a look, then disconnected. Full write-up:

**All six wired sites live at once, clean scorable signal, first unambiguous EMG on this
project.** ⚠️ Changes five variables at once ⇒ reference anchor, **not** an experiment, and
no duty cycle was measured. Found a **180 Hz third harmonic** (25.9% of power on frontal
EEG) that the 60 Hz notch does not touch, and confirmed the **Allego recording path points
into the Obsidian vault**.

> [!warning] Impedance thresholds do not transfer between preps
> On that mouse the two channels reading "open" gave the **largest** signal, and the 26
> **unwired** channels read a median of 0.17 MΩ, better than the real electrodes, because
> unused inputs are tied to ground. Our 2 MΩ rule is calibrated for epidural screws on a
> bipolar RHD2216 and means nothing outside that context.
