# 2026-06-21

> [!abstract] Purpose
> Evidence pack for the professor: **the current wiring is grounded wrong**, and that — not the two-mice idea — is why the signal looks bad. Bring this before doing mouse grounding tomorrow. Full guide: [[signal-quality-and-grounding]]. Bring-up record: [[first-headstage-connection]].

## The claim, in one line
The green **animal-ground wire is soldered to signal pad 5** on the C3410 adapter — an amplifier **input**, not ground. So the animal is **floating**, not grounded. That is the defect to fix.

---

## Proof 1 — the ground wire is physically on the wrong pad
- C3410 adapter has **dedicated `REF` and `GND` pads**, separate from the numbered signal pads (0–31).
- Current build: green animal-ground wire → **signal pad 5** (= native channel `pri_5` / `pri_13`, an amplifier input).
- A signal pad measures voltage; it does **not** tie the animal to the chip's ground. → animal has **no ground reference**.
- **Correct:** green wire on the **`GND` pad**. One animal-ground per setup.

## Proof 2 — live measurements show the floating-animal signature
Signal Metrics, 2026-06-19 (Mouse 1, streaming):

| Channel | Reading | Healthy range | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| EEG+ | ~60–100 µV | 20–300 µV | OK |
| EMG+ | ~60–100 µV | ~10–50 µV rest | OK-ish |
| **EMG−** | **~11.6 mV pk-pk — pegged at the rail** | < ~1–2 mV | 🔴 **saturated** |
| green "ground" channel | **railing** | (should be quiet ground) | 🔴 **floating** |

- Amplifier window = **±5 mV (≈10 mV pk-pk max)**. A channel at **~11.6 mV pk-pk is slammed against the rail = saturated = unusable.**
- The wire we *call* ground is itself **railing** — the textbook "animal is floating" fingerprint.

## Proof 3 — the physics says this is expected
- RHD amplifier only sees **±5 mV**. With no ground, the whole animal drifts out of that window → traces **slam top/bottom (saturate)** and pick up large **50 Hz wall hum**.
- So the bad signal is **predicted** by the missing-ground wiring, not by the two-mice sharing.

## Proof 4 — the fix is a wire move, not a redesign (RHD2216 specifics)
- Our chip = **RHD2216**, a **bipolar** chip: each channel already measures its own `+` − `−` pair, so it is **its own reference**.
- Bipolar ⇒ **no reference-electrode input.** The `REF` pad belongs to *unipolar* RHD2132 only → **leave `REF` empty.**
- It **does** need **GND**. Fix = **move the green wire from signal pad 5 → `GND` pad.** Nothing else changes.

---

## Why this matters for the professor's question (two mice, one headstage)
> [!important] Can't judge the two-mice setup yet
> While channels saturate from a missing ground, **saturation hides everything** — cross-talk between the mice can't be fairly evaluated. **Ground first, then look.** Judging now = judging noise.
