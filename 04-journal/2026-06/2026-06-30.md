## 4. First 2-mice-per-headstage plug 🟡🔴

First time running **2 mice on one headstage**. Told the story in order:

### 4a. Stream — M1 clean, M2 bad
> [!note] Allego state keeps slipping
> Channel selection got wiped (Allego never retains it — [[allego-presets-workspace]]) and the sample rate bounced (30000 ↔ 625). **Reselect channels + set 1000 every time.**

| ntv | signal | RMS µV | read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | M1 EEG | 58 / 115 | ✅ clean — **shared ground works for M1** |
| 2,3 | M1 EMG | railing | open (known) |
| 8 | M2 EEG_F | 4565 | 🔴 railing |
| 9–11 | M2 EEG_P / EMG | 1286 / 679 / 1153 | ⚠️ elevated, not clean |

### 4b. Root cause M2 — dried blood in the socket
On removing M2's head-electrode socket stopper, **brown powder = dried blood from surgery** came out; it was tethered over. Dried blood = resistive crud on the connector pins → high contact impedance → M2 inputs float/rail (fouled ground pin → M2 floats; fouled recording pins → open).

> [!caution] Professor's counter-point (2026-06-30)
> The contact between electrode and pin is long, so surface blood *shouldn't* insulate it. So blood is **plausible but unproven** — clean it, but don't bank on it as the sole cause.

> [!tip] New workflow rule
> **Inspect + clean every implant connector socket for dried blood/debris BEFORE tethering.** Cleaning: dry air/brush, then IPA ≥90 % on the **connector contacts only** (never the skull/wound), dry fully. Added to the plug checklist + [[grounding-reference-noise-concepts]].

### 4c. Headstage dropped → recovered (loose HDMI)
Mid-session `port_num_channels = {}` (server up, mode still correct) → **physical SPI drop**, not a dead headstage. Cause found: the **HDMI adapter (SAdpt → XDAQ) wasn't seated.** Reseated → 32 ch back, live. Lesson: a bumped connector during handling drops enumeration; reseat the SPI/HDMI chain before suspecting the headstage. Ref: [[intan-headstage-troubleshooting]].

### 4d. After recovery — signals degraded, M1 went bad too
**M2 full wiring map captured** (slip-ring colour → adapter pad): `8 red EEG_F_L · 9 black EEG_P_L · 10 yellow EMG · 11 blue EMG · 12 brown EEG_F_R · 13 purple blank · 14 orange blank · green EEG_P_R → GND bus`. So M2 records natives 8,9,10,11,12 (EEG bipolar 8−9, EMG 10−11, pad 12 spare); green (parietal-R) = animal ground. *(Not yet added to `xdat_io.py CHANNEL_MAPS` — wait for clean signal.)*

| ntv | signal | RMS µV | pk-pk µV | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | M1_EEG_F | 4743 | 12779 | 🔴 railing — **was clean 58 µV before the drop** |
| 1 | M1_EEG_P | 154 | 2510 | ~ok |
| 2,3 | M1_EMG | ~4800 | ~12000 | open (known) |
| 8 | M2_EEG_F | 4632 | 12552 | 🔴 railing |
| 9 | M2_EEG_P | 1918 | 11165 | 🔴 railing |
| 10 | M2_EMG_L | 1174 | 7776 | high |
| 11 | M2_EMG_R | 1834 | 10149 | 🔴 railing |
| 12 | M2_EEG_FR | 892 | 3609 | high |

**Diagnosis = flaky CONNECTOR CONTACTS, not the shared ground.** Smoking gun: M1_EEG_F was clean 58 µV, now rails — nothing changed for M1 except the rig was handled. A good electrode going open after handling = disturbed contact at the slip-ring/socket/tether. It's **not** a clean common-mode float (M1_EEG_P still 154 µV, not railing) → individual contacts dropping, not the bus → GND. Session through-line = **unreliable/dirty connector contacts** (M2 blood + handling knocking M1 loose). Can't debug electrically while contacts are intermittent.

### 4e. Open question — can 2 mice share one GND?
Professor's position (matches Intan): an **RHD2216 has one GND pad** → two animals on one chip can't have independent grounds; standard practice = **one ground/reference per animal**. Our data pushes back a little: **M1 read genuinely clean *with* the shared ground engaged**, so it's not flatly impossible — the M1-clean/M2-bad asymmetry looks M2-specific, not a fundamental shared-ground failure.

> [!question] Decision for tomorrow — separate-chip grounding
> If the 32-channel headstage is **two RHD2216 chips (2×16)**, put **M1 on chip 1 (natives 0–15) and M2 on chip 2 (natives 16–31)** → each animal gets its **own GND pad** = independent grounds, Intan-compliant, no coupling. The current `2mice32` map mistakenly puts both mice on chip 1 (M1 = 0–3, M2 = 8–11) → forced to share one GND. **Verify chip count** (headstage part number; RHD2216 = 16 ch → a 32-ch port = 2 of them). If 1 chip → one headstage per mouse. Ref: [[intan-rhd2216-reference]].
