# 2026-07-16 — I have to take back yesterday's discovery, and the real problem is the electrodes

## TL;DR

Yesterday I logged "FIRST SLEEP BIOLOGY EVER RECORDED" in big letters. **It was wrong and I am retracting it.** The channel was saturated against the amplifier rail, and my analysis script was scoring the saturation instead of the mouse. When I reject the clipped data properly, the effect doesn't just weaken, it **flips sign**. There is still zero scoreable sleep in this project.

The overnight run (22:47 to 09:00, 7 mice, foil on) is **0 out of 7**.

BUT, and this is the part that matters, chasing that bug led straight to the actual root cause, and it is not hum, not earth, not shielding. **Every frontal EEG electrode is open on every mouse.** They are not touching brain. Andrea confirmed you cannot reseat an electrode on an implanted mouse, you make new mice, so **this is a surgery problem and the current cohort is spent for frontal EEG.**

Foil is staying, it genuinely works, but we are NOT grounding it, that idea is dead too (details below).

## 1. The retraction, in detail

Yesterday's headline was A1 of `uid0715-18-58-54`, td_sep = -0.65, which I called the first real sleep signal in the project's history.

Here is what I got wrong. I reported "A1 clips 19.3%" as a footnote caveat, as if roughly one epoch in five was bad. **That is not what the number meant.** 19.3% was the MEDIAN WITHIN-EPOCH clip fraction, meaning the typical epoch had 19.3% of its samples slammed against the rail. The actual count of clean epochs:

> [!danger] Only **1 of 493 epochs** on A1 had zero clipped samples.

The raw voltages confirm it beyond argument. A1's frontal leg hits `+6389.6 µV` against a `6390 µV` rail, with a standard deviation of about `4050 µV`. Real mouse bipolar EEG is under `300 µV`. That channel was not recording a mouse, it was pinned to the rail.

Then the threshold sweep, which is the bit that ends the discussion:

| clip threshold | epochs kept | td_sep |
|---|---|---|
| 100% (no rejection) | 493 | **-0.65** ← what I reported |
| <20% clipped | 397 | -0.66 |
| **<10% clipped** | **150** | **+0.63** ← SIGN FLIPS |
| <5% clipped | 60 | +0.64 |

The "sleep" **reverses the instant you exclude railed epochs.** A saturated amplifier is non-linear, so its spectrum is meaningless, and clipping severity happens to covary with EMG, which manufactures a perfect fake NREM signature. I built a metric that measured the artifact and then congratulated myself on it.

> [!quote] Paper-ready
> Epochs containing amplifier saturation were excluded prior to spectral analysis. Retaining saturated epochs inverted the apparent theta/delta separation between low- and high-EMG states (Cliff's delta -0.65 vs +0.63), demonstrating that clipping artifact alone can produce a spurious sleep-like signature.

### The second bug, which I made this morning while fixing the first

When I added clipped-epoch rejection, slots with too few survivors silently fell back to the dirty array and **printed a verdict from it anyway**. That is how C1 got a bogus "SLEEP-LIKE -0.26" out of 3 usable epochs. `state_scan.py` now hard-refuses with `UNSCOREABLE (only N clean epochs)` and never falls back.

### Lessons burned into the tooling

- **Reject clipped epochs BEFORE computing any metric.** Not as a caveat afterwards.
- **Always report `keep%`** (fraction of epochs that are actually clean), never a median rail% on its own. Those two numbers are catastrophically easy to confuse and I confused them in public.
- A false positive is worse than a null. The null would have cost us a day. The false positive nearly went into a paper.

## 3. What actually rails the channels, and why it exonerates earth AND foil-grounding

I decomposed the raw legs (2 min at hour 2, single-ended):

```
slot leg      DC_uV    AC_uV  60Hz_uV  <1Hz_uV  rail%
A1   EEG_F       -0     4076     4073       11   16.8%
A1   EEG_P       -1     4301     4292       21   18.2%
A2   EEG_F       +0     3785     3752       28   10.8%
A2   EEG_P       +0      161       12       95    0.0%   <-- 12 uV !!
B1   EEG_F       +1     4270     4262       17   12.5%
B1   EEG_P       -2      257       80      116    0.0%   <-- 80 uV
D1   EEG_F       -0       95       89        0    0.0%   <-- 89 uV
D1   EEG_P       -0     2865     2677       24    2.3%
```

Two things fall out of this and they are both decisive.

**First, DC offset is ZERO everywhere.** So the railing is not drift, not a bad reference, not a gain misconfiguration. It is **pure 60 Hz**, 4 mV of it, on specific legs only.

**Second, and this is the whole ballgame:**

> [!success] A2 frontal reads **3752 µV** of 60 Hz. A2 parietal reads **12 µV**.
> Same mouse. Same headstage. Same foil. Two electrodes millimetres apart. **A 300-fold difference.**

No shielding story can explain that. A shield is a global object, it cannot protect one leg and abandon its neighbour. So:

- **The foil WORKS.** The 12 µV, 46 µV, 80 µV, 89 µV legs are the proof. Under the floating foil, the common-mode floor is already at or below our <50-100 µV target.
- **Earth is exonerated.** Not the binding variable. Never was. The earthed-brick trial on 07-15 already showed no effect, and now we know why, there was nothing left for it to fix.
- **Grounding the foil is DEAD.** Andrea called this, and the data agrees. I had claimed we were "leaving ~40 dB unclaimed" by leaving the foil floating. That was theory. Measurement says the floor is already low, so there is no 40 dB to claim. And a grounded shield cannot possibly fix a 300× spread *inside itself*. On top of that, our Gen-1 XDAQ Core has no chassis ground terminal to tie a shield to anyway. **Dropped, and logged as dropped so nobody re-proposes it.**

This is the council's equation with the variables finally pinned down:

> `differential_60Hz ≈ common_mode(floor) × electrode_Z_imbalance`
>
> Foil crushed the first term. **The second term is now the entire problem.**

## 4. The impedance measurement, and the free control we got by accident

Andrea exported impedance at 10:12 with only A (2 mice) and B (1 mouse) connected. Note, the Allego export dumps **every port whether or not a mouse is attached**, so the file contained C and D too. That accident is a gift.

> [!tip] The disconnected ports are an empirical open-circuit calibration
> **C and D had NO MICE.** Their electrodes were floating in air, and they read **2.5 to 7 MΩ** with wild positive phase. So on this rig, *"connected to nothing"* = 2.5-7 MΩ. **Any leg on a real mouse reading in that band is not touching tissue.** We have never had that reference number before, and now we do.

Phase gives an independent read on the same thing. A real electrode-electrolyte interface is capacitive, so phase should be near 0° or negative. Anything up at +40° to +180° is **nonphysical** and means open.

The three real mice:

```
A1   EEG_F  8.698 MOhm  phase +125  OPEN        EEG_P  1.094  +5.9   weak but real    imbalance 8.0x   FAIL
A2   EEG_F  3.921 MOhm  phase +158  floating    EEG_P  5.316  +30    OPEN             both dead        FAIL
B1   EEG_F  7.641 MOhm  phase +46   OPEN        EEG_P  0.206  +1.1   GOOD             imbalance 37x    FAIL
```

Impedance and phase agree, and both agree with the recording. **B1 frontal at 7.641 MΩ picked up 4262 µV. B1 parietal at 0.206 MΩ picked up 80 µV.** A 53× impedance ratio produced a 53× hum ratio. The mechanism is no longer a hypothesis, it is a measured proportionality.

## 5. The real finding, every frontal electrode is open on every mouse

> [!danger] 🔴 ALL THREE frontal EEG electrodes are open. A1, A2, B1. All of them. Every animal.
> That is not bad luck, three-for-three is a systematic failure of one electrode position.

Andrea confirms the frontal is **the same wire** as the parietal, nothing electrically different about it. So the failure is anatomical or surgical, not electrical.

**LEAD HYPOTHESIS, unproven, for the next cohort: the frontal sinus.** Mice have an air cavity under the frontal bone. A pin placed over it lands in air and never reaches dura, giving a permanent open circuit. This would explain the pattern exactly, frontal only, every animal, same wire, high Z, nonphysical phase. The parietal has no sinus under it and the parietal is our one consistently good leg. Testable at the next surgery by moving the frontal placement off the sinus and by checking DMM continuity per leg before closing.

> [!danger] 🔴 CORRECTION, an implanted electrode CANNOT be reseated
> I had suggested reseating leads and cleaning sockets as a fix. **Andrea, 2026-07-16: that is not a thing, you cannot reseat an electrode on an implanted mouse, you make new mice.** So a dead frontal electrode means a spent animal. Every mouse currently on the rig is already lost for frontal EEG. **The fix is only available in the next surgical cohort**, which makes surgical yield the project's critical path, not electronics.
