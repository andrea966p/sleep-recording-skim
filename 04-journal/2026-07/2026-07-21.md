# 2026-07-21 — Grounded the one good electrode by accident, trial failed

## TL;DR

Andrea re-tethered mouse **MB1** (port B, slot 1) and ran two QC recordings. The tether change put the **green animal-ground wire onto the SAME left-parietal dura pin** as the black EEG− wire. That physically **shorted left parietal to the GND bus**, so we lost our one good electrode on this animal. Both recordings came back **NOT SCORABLE**, same faults, longer recording did not help because the wiring never changed. We convened the LLM council to troubleshoot, and walked through exactly where grounding lives (hardware, not software) and why Allego CAR cannot save this. Fix is at the soldering iron, not in software. Still zero scoreable EEG.

Parietal was the **only quiet pad in the entire 8-mouse file** both times. Every other mouse's parietal reads 1900–4600 µV (open). That single quiet pad is the tell, it is quiet because it is tied to ground, not because it is a clean brain electrode. It even drifted 123 → 245 µV between trials, which is a wandering ground node, not a stable electrode.

## The mistake

The wiring this trial:

- black wire (EEG−) → left parietal dura pin. Correct.
- green wire (animal ground → GND pad of the adapter board) → **also soldered onto that same left parietal pin**.

So left parietal is now electrically the same node as animal ground.

> [!warning] NEVER share a recording-electrode pin with the animal-ground wire
> On our rig every pad is recorded single-ended as `pad − GND`. If you solder GND onto a signal pin, that channel becomes `GND − GND ≈ 0`. You do not record that electrode, you DELETE it. No filter, no re-reference, no software can recover a channel that was physically clamped to ground during acquisition.

Why the bipolar EEG is garbage: `EEG = frontal − parietal` = open-frontal (a 1.5 mV mains antenna) minus a near-AC-ground. That is maximum electrode-impedance imbalance, which by our own relation `differential_60Hz ≈ common_mode × Z_imbalance` produces the worst-case hum. We hand-built the worst possible EEG.

## Where grounding actually lives (the thing Andrea asked)

Three separate layers, and grounding is only in the first one:

1. **Animal grounding = HARDWARE / physical.** Green wire → GND pad → RHD2216 analog ground. Every amplifier records `electrode − GND`. This sets the zero and keeps tissue potential inside the amp's common-mode range. This is the layer we broke. Unfixable in post.
2. **Intan RHD2216 chip.** Digitizes each pad single-ended vs that ground. Despite the "bipolar chip" name, the chip does NOT difference frontal-vs-parietal on-chip. REF pad empty by design. Nothing configurable here for this problem.
3. **The bipolar EEG = OFFLINE post-process.** `dsp.py bipolar()` computes `frontal − parietal` in Python, step 1 of the pipeline. This is our real reference. Flexible across channels, but only across channels that actually recorded brain.

Allego CAR (Common Average Reference) sits as an optional **software** layer (Radiens Signal Processing → Reference Types, manual p68–70), and we keep it **OFF** by rule: it cross-contaminates the bipolar ± pairs and, with mostly-open channels, would inject the open frontals' 60 Hz into everything. CAR is a many-good-channels tool. MB1 has zero good EEG channels right now, so no software referencing of any kind helps. See [[04-processing-guidelines]] §2.

## What the council concluded

Convened all five advisors + peer review. High-confidence agreement:

- Self-inflicted short, unanimous. 123/245 µV = grounded node, not brain.
- Longer recording cannot help, time does not heal a short.
- The 28 s vs 30 s "early stop" is a **red herring** — grounding a channel cannot stop the DAQ clock. 28.032 s = a manual/rounded stop. Not a physics event.
- EMG railed is a **separate** fault (EMG− DC-offset/intermittent), needs its own DMM continuity check.

Where they clashed: is MB1 salvageable, or new-mice-only? Contrarian said new-mice-only (frontal open = no valid pair ever). Executor/Expansionist/First-Principles said salvageable IF a second real intracranial contact exists — and it plausibly does: the green wire normally lands on **parietal-RIGHT dura**, a real implanted contact currently wasted as ground. Peer review flagged the dangerous idea (declaring "just switch to single-ended vs REF" easy — REF is empty by design, that needs a hardware rewire, not a software toggle) and the real risk (desoldering green off the shared pin could lift/damage the one good parietal contact).

## The fix (bench, in order) — NOT done yet

1. **Measure BEFORE touching the iron.** Allego impedance mode, map every pin as-is. Confirm parietal-left is the shorted 123/245 µV node, confirm parietal-right / green pin still reads 0.2–1.1 MΩ (the salvage partner), confirm frontal still open. Protect the good electrode from a blind iron.
2. Desolder green OFF the parietal pin. Green goes to its OWN dedicated ground only (parietal-right dura or a fresh skull screw). Never share a signal pin again.
3. Re-QC, verify parietal-left impedance recovers to 0.2–1.1 MΩ.
4. Salvage montage: drop frontal entirely (it is a permanently open sinus lottery, this is now the 4th mouse confirming the wall). Re-reference EEG to **parietal-L vs parietal-R** bipolar, both good dura contacts. Delta/theta NREM/REM separation survives on one good EEG lead + EMG.
5. EMG: DMM continuity on yellow/blue, reseat, confirm < 2000 µV before trusting.
