# 2026-07-15

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> Three things today. First, the RustDesk plus HDMI-switch crash fix is CONFIRMED WORKING, a remote user connected, the screen flickered for under a second, and the desktop session survived where it used to collapse and kill Allego. Second, I QC'd the morning port A recordings (2 mice) and they were unusable, 60 Hz hum all over them, so I summoned a 5-advisor LLM council to figure out the earth and the sleep data with full scientific rigor and zero bias. ## 1. Display fix VALIDATED (RustDesk plus HDMI-switch crash)

The GRUB boot parameter I staged on 2026-07-14 (`video=HDMI-A-1:1920x1080@60e`, forces the HDMI connector permanently enabled so a monitor switch cannot hotplug its EDID) is now proven to work.

Boot 2026-07-14 19:10 with the parameter active. On 2026-07-15 09:53 a remote person connected over RustDesk, they saw the screen flicker for less than a second, then kept using it normally. The logs confirm the session SURVIVED:

- `gnome-shell` stayed at PID 2730, up continuously since boot (14 hours 49 minutes elapsed) straight through the RustDesk connect. The old bug spawned a NEW PID because the whole session collapsed to the login screen. That did not happen.
- NO GDM (GNOME Display Manager) restart, NO segfault, NO `gtk_main_quit / main_loops` assertion, NO `50051: Connection refused` for Allego. Only harmless CRON session-close noise.
- `xrandr` shows HDMI-1 locked at 1920x1080 primary, stayed `connected`. The kernel log shows NO EDID re-probe storm. The sub-second flicker was ONE clean modeset, not the storm that used to crash things.

The old failure chain (switch flip, then EDID re-probe storm, then RustDesk `--server` crash, then X11 session collapse, then Allego dies) is broken at step one. This is marked DONE in [[2026-07-13]].

> [!warning] Still not proven, acquisition DURING a flip. The flip test ran with no live recording (the morning clips had already ended). Session survival, the thing that actually killed Allego, is confirmed. Do not stress-test a monitor switch in the middle of a real multi-day run just to see.

## 3. The council, what it did and what it found

I convened a 5-advisor LLM council, same method as the [[2026-05-01-llm-council-review]], full detail and provenance in the decision doc [[2026-07-15-earth-and-sleep-council]]. The point was absolute rigor and no bias, so I gave each advisor the SAME raw evidence packet (all the QC across 2026-07-10 to 07-15, the hardware facts, and the competing hypotheses framed as genuinely open), told them to re-derive from scratch and NOT trust the vault's prior conclusions, and dispatched them in parallel with no cross-talk so they could not groupthink. Three of the five wrote their own analysis scripts and recomputed everything straight from the raw `.xdat` files instead of trusting my summary numbers.

The five lenses, electrical first-principles, contrarian null-hypothesis, signal metrologist, bench experimentalist, and sleep-EEG scoring. Then I synthesized as chairman.

### What they agreed on (5 of 5)

- **No scoreable sleep data exists yet, across ANY recording.** The only genuinely physiological trace in the whole dataset is one roughly 42 second clip (A2 of `uid0714-16-58-16`, delta-dominant real cortical EEG), far too short to stage sleep. Everything else is unusable.
- **The hum is DIFFERENTIAL at the amplifier, so a notch plus bipolar CANNOT rescue it.** The metrologist proved the buried biology is not just hidden, it is ABSENT, the connected mouse A2 on 07-15 is spectrally identical to an open wire-to-nowhere channel.
- **The floating slots B, C, D are NOT a valid "earth control".** Open unconnected inputs pick up 60 Hz no matter what the system earth is doing, so their loudness tells you nothing about the earth. This was an error in my own evidence packet, all five caught it independently.
- **Per-electrode impedance was never measured before the 07-15 runs, and it is the cheapest thing that would settle the argument.** (I ran it, see section 4.)

### The root cause the council converged on

It is BOTH earth and contact, and the two "camps" were measuring two factors of one product, not disagreeing:

```
differential_60Hz  ~  common_mode_mains(earth floor)  x  electrode_impedance_imbalance(contact)
```

- The common-mode mains field is REAL and present, one single source drives every channel (phase-locking value 1.00 across all slots), and a connected electrode sat at roughly 3 mV of single-ended 60 Hz, which a solidly-earthed electrode physically cannot do. So the system is still effectively floating, that sets the FLOOR.
- But the montage does not reject that field, because electrode-impedance imbalance converts it to a differential signal (bipolar-to-common-mode 60 Hz ratio about 2). The electrode contact is the BINDING, controllable variable, same earth, same instant, one channel clean and another dirty depending only on its contact.

The honest disagreement was the relative weight of earth versus contact, and one advisor (the contrarian) raised a hypothesis nobody else did and that I had not considered, that the electrician's new permanent wall-earth bond may have created a GROUND LOOP, which would explain why 07-15 is actually WORSE than the pre-fix floating baseline. That is a live minority possibility, roughly 20%, and a single earth-lift test settles it. The display, RustDesk, and analysis-artifact hypotheses were rejected, the hum was present before RustDesk even connected.

### What the council forced me to retract (this is the no-bias payoff)

The council caught my OWN vault overstating things, and I corrected them:

- "Earthing fix VALIDATED / paper-ready 2026-07-14" was OVERSTATED. It rested on one 42 second single-electrode clip, with the other electrode dirty in the same instant, and that electrode swung bad within minutes. That proves a matched electrode rejects common-mode, it does NOT prove the earth fix works system-wide. Correction callout added to [[2026-07-14]].
- The "matched before/after pair" was NOT a controlled comparison, two variables changed at once.
- The QC metric `60Hz%raw` is denominator-confounded and should not be the primary number, use absolute microvolts instead. And `multimouse_scan.py` has two real statistical bugs (it concatenates 40 overlapping windows before the Welch spectrum, understating variance about 20x, and the sub-minute clips only have 2 to 4 effectively-independent windows, not 40). Also its USABLE threshold is too loose and never checks EMG (electromyography), so a USABLE verdict only certifies Wake and NREM, never REM, unless EMG is separately validated.

## 4. TEST 1, the impedance, integrated (H2 CONFIRMED)

After the council I ran the per-electrode impedance sweep, port A, 2 mice, GUI-paused (`impedance port A 2 mice.csv`). Under the sleep64 map, mouse A1 is sites A/1 to A/4 and mouse A2 is sites A/33 to A/36, in the order EEG frontal, EEG parietal, EMG left, EMG right. The gate is 0.1 to 1 MΩ good, above 5 MΩ open, and the two legs of a pair within 2 to 3 times of each other.

| mouse | leg | site | magnitude | phase | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | EEG frontal | A/1 | 0.824 MΩ | 175 deg | GOOD |
| A1 | EEG parietal | A/2 | **7.144 MΩ** | 138 deg | **OPEN** |
| A1 | EMG left | A/3 | 2.148 MΩ | 168 deg | mediocre |
| A1 | EMG right | A/4 | 2.414 MΩ | 167 deg | mediocre (matched to A/3) |
| A2 | EEG frontal | A/33 | **8.927 MΩ** | 57 deg | **OPEN** |
| A2 | EEG parietal | A/34 | **8.416 MΩ** | 128 deg | **OPEN** |
| A2 | EMG left | A/35 | 0.665 MΩ | 176 deg | good |
| A2 | EMG right | A/36 | 2.697 MΩ | 165 deg | mediocre (imbalanced 4x vs A/35) |

The read is unambiguous, NOT ONE EEG bipolar pair on port A has two good matched legs:

- **A1 has one open leg**, frontal is good at 0.82 MΩ but parietal is open at 7.14 MΩ, an 8.7x imbalance. One open leg kills the bipolar, and the open high-impedance parietal leg acts as the 60 Hz antenna, which matches the contrarian's finding that A1's parietal dominated its pickup.
- **A2 has BOTH EEG legs open**, 8.9 and 8.4 MΩ, no usable EEG contact at all, which is why A2 was the worst HUM in every QC.

Sanity check passes, the grounded floor sites (A/17 to 32 and A/49 to 64, around 0.04 to 0.2 MΩ, phase near 0 or 180) read exactly as they should, so the amplifier and the measurement itself are fine. The failure is SPECIFICALLY the electrode contacts.

The short recording taken right after the sweep (`uid0715-10-28-40`, 1.5 min, byte-perfect) confirmed it, A1 at phys 10% MARGINAL and A2 at phys 10% HUM, unchanged, because I only measured, I did not reseat anything. A1's frontal leg was even railing 11%, probably nudged during the sweep.

> [!important] What TEST 1 settles and what it does NOT. It CONFIRMS the contact problem (hypothesis H2) is real and by itself enough to explain the port A hum. It does NOT clear the earth (H1), because with legs this open, bad contact alone produces the differential hum, so the earth floor cannot be judged until the contacts are fixed. The earth still has to be tested (TEST 3, short EEG frontal and parietal to the headstage ground pad and record, if a shorted input STILL shows millivolt 60 Hz the problem is upstream common-mode, if it goes quiet it is electrode impedance).

## 5. Splitter-bypass trial (after lunch, ~13:25) — SPI splitter RULED OUT

Quick control to rule out the passive SPI splitter as a hum source. I took the SAME two mice that gave the bad morning runs on port A and re-cabled them with NO splitter, just SPI straight to the headstage adapter, 4 channels each. Mouse 1 (this morning's port-A "A1") stayed on port A. Mouse 2 (this morning's "A2") moved to port B on its own SPI cable. Impedance files `only_spi_A1.csv` and `only_spi_B1.csv`, recording `uid0715-13-25-09` (0.88 min, byte-perfect). Without the splitter each port enumerates 32 channels, so port A = data columns 0 to 3 and port B = columns 32 to 35 (confirmed from the JSON `sensors_by_port`, `connection_ntv_idx` 0 and 32).

**Impedance, same mice, EEG legs (frontal / parietal):**

| mouse | config | EEG_F | EEG_P | read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 (was "A1") | WITH splitter (AM) | 0.824 | 7.144 MΩ | F good, P open |
| M1, port A | NO splitter | 7.018 | 7.016 MΩ | both open |
| M2 (was "A2") | WITH splitter (AM) | 8.927 | 8.416 MΩ | both open |
| M2, port B | NO splitter | 2.132 | 0.116 MΩ | F high, P good |

**Recording QC, same mice (60 Hz raw to notch / residual / phys%):**

| mouse | config | 60Hz | resid | phys | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | splitter (port A) | 75 to 17% | 386 µV | 10% | MARGINAL |
| M1 | NO splitter (port A) | 81 to 10% | 632 µV | 16% | HUM/MARG |
| M2 | splitter (port A) | 92 to 15% | 928 µV | 10% | HUM |
| M2 | NO splitter (port B) | 86 to 18% | 340 µV | 16% | HUM |

The recording confirms the impedance, M1 both legs about 2900 µV symmetric (both around 7 MΩ), M2 frontal 3853 µV railing 15% versus parietal 206 µV clean (matches 2.13 versus 0.116 MΩ, one open leg).

> [!check] Conclusion, the splitter is NOT in the causal chain.
> The grounded floor sites read clean 0.04 to 0.2 MΩ with AND without the splitter, so the splitter does not corrupt the signal path or the impedance measurement. Both mice stay HUM-dominated at phys 16%, unusable, splitter or not, the 60 Hz differential hum is identical. The per-channel differences (M1 got worse, M2 got better) track ELECTRODE IMPEDANCE changes from re-cabling and handling, not the splitter, M2's small gain is just its parietal reseating to 0.116 MΩ while its frontal open leg still kills the bipolar. Removing a passive SPI splitter changes nothing about the hum. **Cross the splitter off the list.** Still H1 floating floor plus H2 contact, per [[2026-07-15-earth-and-sleep-council]].
>
> CAVEATS, the recording is 53 s so it is a spot reading (few independent windows), and the two impedance sweeps disagree on shared sites, meaning contacts drift with handling between sweeps. The no-splitter-effect conclusion holds regardless.
