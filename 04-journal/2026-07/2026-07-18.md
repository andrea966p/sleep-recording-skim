# 2026-07-18 — I was reading EMG wrong, and the good news is EMG is not our problem

## TL;DR

Andrea pushed back on how I was judging EMG, and Andrea was right. I had been calling EMG legs "railed" off their **single-ended 60Hz amplitude**. That is the wrong test. The scored EMG is the **bipolar difference** EMG_L − EMG_R, which **rejects common-mode 60Hz**, and our reference pin is empty by design so every leg carries a big common-mode hum that cancels in the difference. So a leg showing 4 mV of 60Hz single-ended is NOT a fault. The only thing that actually breaks a differential is a leg **clipping the ADC** (`rail%`), because a saturated amplifier is non-linear and you cannot subtract that back out.

I re-checked current practice, widened the EMG band to the convention (10–150 Hz + 60 Hz notch), added a real EMG-clipping column to the analysis, and re-ran every troubleshooting recording. Result:

- **EMG contact is GOOD on multiple mice** (C2, D1, D2) in every run, clean and live and bimodal. EMG was never the blocker.
- **A2 is the one chronic EMG offender** — its EMG legs clip 17–23% on every single run, even though A2 has our cleanest EEG pair. Opposite failure.
- **Frontal EEG is still the entire wall.** Still zero sleep biology anywhere.

## 1. The mistake, and why it is a mistake

Yesterday and this morning I flagged EMG legs as railed because each leg showed 3–5 mV of 60 Hz single-ended. Wrong reasoning, for a specific hardware reason.

> [!important] Why single-ended 60Hz is NOT a fault on this rig
> Our RHD2216 runs bipolar with the REF pin **empty by design**. With no reference return, every electrode leg floats up on the full common-mode mains hum. That common-mode is **identical on both legs of a pair**, so it **cancels** when we take the bipolar difference (EMG_L − EMG_R, EEG_F − EEG_P). A leg reading 4 mV of 60 Hz single-ended can still give a perfectly clean differential.

The thing that genuinely destroys a differential is **clipping**. If the common-mode is large enough to slam a leg against the ±6390 µV rail, that leg is non-linear for those samples, and a non-linear term does not subtract away. So the correct per-leg fault test is `rail%` (fraction of samples pinned to the rail), NOT the 60 Hz amplitude.

> [!quote] Paper-ready
> Because the amplifier reference was left floating, each electrode carried a large common-mode mains component that was rejected by the bipolar derivation; single-ended 60 Hz amplitude therefore does not index contact quality. The operative per-electrode fault is amplifier saturation, which is non-linear and survives common-mode rejection.

This also sharpens the contact model: an **open / high-impedance leg floats toward the full common-mode swing and rails**, while a well-contacted leg is clamped near tissue potential and stays inside the rail. So `rail%` per leg IS the contact readout, and it is consistent with the council's `differential_60Hz ≈ common_mode × Z_imbalance`.

## 2. What I changed in the tooling

- **`state_scan.py` EMG band widened 10–100 → 10–150 Hz + 60 Hz notch**, the standard rodent-EMG convention (Rayan 2024 EJN; Sensors 2025 vigilance-state algorithm). EMG amplitude feature stays RMS of the differential.
- **EMG now runs at full `fs` (1000 Hz), not decimated.** The old code decimated by 4 to `fsd = 250 Hz`, whose Nyquist is 125 Hz, so a 150 Hz band edge would have been illegal. EMG gets its own full-rate notch comb + bandpass; EEG stays on the decimated path.
- **Added `EMGrl%` column** (median per-epoch EMG-leg clipping) so EMG contact is visible per slot. Previously the verdict only ever looked at EEG rail.
- Also fixed a latent `np.trapezoid` → `np.trapz` (this box runs numpy 1.26, `trapezoid` is a numpy 2.0 name — the script would have crashed on any run here).

## 4. The A2 paradox

```
A2 EMGrl% :  22.8% (0715-18)  →  18.1% (0715-22)  →  19.6% (0717)  →  16.6% (0718)
A2 EEG    :  clean pair, 77 uV then 80 uV differential, 0% EEG-leg clip today
```

A2's **EMG legs clip on every run**, yet A2 has our **cleanest EEG pair**. Same headstage, same mouse. So contact quality is per-electrode, not per-animal: A2's EEG electrodes are seated, its EMG electrodes are open/floating and rail on common-mode. Exactly the inverse of the frontal-EEG mice, which nails down that these are independent electrode-placement outcomes, not a systemic electrical fault.

## 5. What did NOT change

- **Frontal EEG is still the whole wall.** `keep%` (EEG-clean epochs) is what sits near zero, never `EMGrl%`.
- **Still zero sleep biology.** Every `td_sep` is positive or NaN after clean-epoch rejection — even on C2/D1/D2 with textbook EMG. Good EMG is necessary, not sufficient; the bipolar EEG has to be alive too, and on the frontal-open mice it is not.
- Low `EEGuV` (7–95 µV) on some slots is **ambiguous** — well-clamped contact vs a shorted/dead pair reading near zero look the same in voltage. Only impedance separates them, which is another vote for the [[#Still open|pre-run impedance gate]].
